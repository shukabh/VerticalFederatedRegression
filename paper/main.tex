\begin{abstract}
We study two-party vertical federated least squares between a researcher $\Rp$, who holds a cohort with features $\bXR$, and an organization $\Op$ (for example a national statistical agency), which holds a register with features $\bXO$ and the response $\by$. Records are linked by labeled private set intersection with an oblivious pseudorandom function: $\Rp$ learns which of its cohort members are in $\Op$'s register and where, and $\Op$ learns only public sizes. The statistics that depend on $\Op$'s data are aggregated under two-party threshold CKKS. The secret key is split additively between the parties, so neither can decrypt alone, and $\Op$ never decrypts at all. Instead, $\Op$ adds the differential-privacy noise inside its share of each decryption, as an exact discrete Gaussian on the coefficient lattice that also floods the encryption error. $\Rp$ therefore receives only noisy statistics, and $\Op$ can enforce a privacy budget by withholding its share. $\Rp$'s own Gram block is computed locally and is never noised. Each run makes two releases. The first, of the Gram blocks only, chooses the ridge. The second, whose noise is independent of that choice, gives the estimate, a Wald interval for the ridge target from a parametric bootstrap of the mechanism, and a bias-aware interval for the regression coefficients. We give the exact replace-one sensitivity, zCDP accounting with a tight conversion, simulation-based output privacy against an honest-but-curious researcher, and the statistical guarantees of the two-stage procedure. In a simulation of the release and solve steps the Wald intervals cover $94.4$--$96.2\%$ (target $95.5\%$) with and without the ridge. On a synthetic population at $\delta = 10^{-9}$, the stage-1 ridge switches off between $1.0$ and $1.2$ times the intersection size that the protocol parameters predict ($6.0$k to $81$k matched records for $\varepsilon = 8$ to $0.5$). The coefficient error then reaches twice that of non-private least squares at $7.3$k, $23$k and $79$k matched records for $\varepsilon = 8$, $4$ and $2$, and prediction comes within $10\%$ of non-private test error $1.7$--$5.2$ times sooner. On real data with each party normalizing its own block of every row, prediction comes within $10\%$ of non-private test error at $10$k and $5.2$k matched records on protein for $\varepsilon = 1$ and $2$, but not within the $12$k--$14$k records available on bike and pol for $\varepsilon \le 4$. On jointly normalized data, AdaSSP on the joined table has lower test error than v3.1 on $20$ of $29$ datasets at $\varepsilon = 1$, $\delta = 10^{-6}$ ($19$ of $29$ when each party normalizes its own block), because the v3.1 ridge, sized for valid intervals, is $3$--$6$ times that of AdaSSP under the same guarantee. End-to-end runs of a full OpenFHE implementation on the protein dataset ($19{,}206$ matched records) reproduce a plaintext simulation of the mechanism and, after each party normalizes its own block of every row, come within $2$--$6\%$ of non-private test error at $\varepsilon \in \{1, 4\}$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ==============================================================

Organizations such as Statistics Canada (StatCan) hold administrative and survey datasets that span the full Canadian population. University researchers, by contrast, often hold smaller cohort datasets enriched with domain-specific variables---clinical measurements, survey responses, or behavioural signals---that StatCan does not collect. Neither dataset alone is sufficient to answer certain research questions: a policy researcher studying labour-market outcomes might hold a cohort's health and education records, while StatCan holds their tax filing history, employment insurance claims, and income trajectories. The scientific value of joining these datasets is evident; the barrier to doing so is equally clear. Privacy law, institutional policy, and the practical realities of data governance make direct data sharing infeasible.

The Research Data Centre (RDC) model partially addresses this by letting researchers work with anonymized StatCan data inside a controlled environment. However, it does not permit a researcher to bring their own dataset and perform a record linkage against StatCan records in order to jointly train a statistical model---a process that typically requires six to eight weeks of vetting, human review, and institutional approvals. This overhead is a meaningful bottleneck on the pace and scope of Canadian policy research.

This document proposes a cryptographic protocol for \emph{vertical federated learning} (VFL) between a researcher and StatCan. The term ``vertical'' refers to the structure of the data: both parties hold overlapping sets of individuals (rows) but non-overlapping sets of variables (columns). The goal is to jointly fit a regression model over the combined column space without either party ever observing the other's raw data. The two datasets are linked by a common identifier, but the linkage itself must remain private: StatCan should not learn which of its records belong to the researcher's cohort.

\paragraph{Scope.} We treat the case in which \textbf{StatCan holds the response variable} $\by$ (e.g.\ tax-reported income, employment status, benefit receipt), and the researcher wishes to learn whether their cohort-specific covariates predict that outcome, using StatCan's covariates as controls. Both parties are honest-but-curious.

\paragraph{The design in one paragraph.} Linkage is labeled private set intersection (PSI) on oblivious-PRF values of the identifiers, after which $\Rp$ knows its matches and their positions in $\Op$'s secretly shuffled register. $\Rp$ computes its own Gram block exactly and uploads its features, aligned to $\Op$'s order and zero elsewhere, encrypted under its share of a two-party CKKS key. $\Op$ converts them to the joint key and multiplies them by its own plaintext columns, which gives encryptions of every statistic that involves its data. A release is a threshold decryption in which $\Op$ computes its share of the decryption, adds discrete-Gaussian noise to it, and sends it without the part of the ciphertext that would let $\Rp$ remove the noise. $\Op$ never decrypts anything, $\Rp$ never sees a noiseless statistic that depends on $\Op$'s data, and every decryption needs $\Op$'s cooperation, so $\Op$ can refuse once the budget is spent.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Protocol.} A two-party protocol that combines OPRF-based labeled PSI, a seeded upload under $\Rp$'s key share converted by $\Op$, plaintext--ciphertext aggregation, and a release in which $\Op$'s decryption share carries the DP noise (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}, Algorithms~\ref{alg:setup}--\ref{alg:sub}).
  \item \textbf{Privacy.} The exact replace-one sensitivity of the released statistics; a lattice discrete-Gaussian release that is exactly $\rho_s$-zCDP and statistically floods every encryption error term; simulation-based output privacy; composition of runs, fixed or adaptively chosen, under a per-register ledger; input and cohort privacy (Sections~\ref{sec:privacy} and~\ref{sec:input}).
  \item \textbf{Parameters.} The protocol and every theorem are stated for a parameter vector with explicit admissibility constraints; \textsc{Calibrate} derives the flooding parameter from the budget and the ledger cap, and the scale and the noise from both (Section~\ref{sec:params}).
  \item \textbf{Statistics.} A two-stage ridge whose choice is independent of the noise in the estimation release; two named estimands; a Wald interval from a parametric bootstrap of the mechanism; a bias-aware interval for the regression coefficients; and the leading-order mechanism bias with an optional correction (Sections~\ref{sec:stats} and~\ref{sec:bias}).
  \item \textbf{Preprocessing.} Per-party transforms with public constants that make typical records fill the clipping bounds, which no joint computation is needed for (Section~\ref{sec:protocol:preproc}).
  \item \textbf{Evaluation.} Exactness of the cryptographic layer, the calibration of the two-release protocol, interval coverage, end-to-end runs of a full implementation on real and synthetic data, coefficient accuracy against the intersection size, and a benchmark against AdaSSP on 29 UCI datasets (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
% ==============================================================

\tocheck{All bibliographic details in this section were written from memory and must be verified. The positioning against Froelicher et al. also needs a careful read of their papers.}

\paragraph{Vertical federated regression.} Linear and logistic regression on vertically partitioned data has been built from additively homomorphic encryption with entity resolution~\cite{hardy2017private} and from secure multiparty computation~\cite{gascon2017privacy}; see~\cite{yang2019federated} for a survey. These systems typically assume a coordinator or reveal the exact fitted model, and they do not give the output a differential-privacy guarantee.

\paragraph{Multiparty and threshold homomorphic encryption.} Threshold fully homomorphic encryption goes back to Asharov et al.~\cite{asharov2012multiparty}. Mouchet et al.~\cite{mouchet2021multiparty} give practical $n$-out-of-$n$ multiparty schemes from RLWE, with collective key generation and collective key switching, which we use with two parties. Froelicher et al.~\cite{froelicher2020drynx,froelicher2021truly} build federated analytics on multiparty HE and add differential-privacy noise when results are collectively decrypted. Our release follows the same principle of attaching the noise to the decryption. Our setting differs: the data are vertically partitioned, records must first be linked privately, one party is the only analyst, and we give the exact sensitivity under the resulting adjacency, the flooding analysis for CKKS, and valid inference.

\paragraph{Differentially private linear regression.} Sufficient-statistics perturbation is the standard approach~\cite{dwork2014dp,sheffet2017differentially}; AdaSSP~\cite{wang2018revisiting} adds an adaptive ridge chosen from a private estimate of the smallest eigenvalue. These methods assume one curator who holds the joined table. Our ridge rule borrows the idea of choosing $\lambda$ from a private eigenvalue bound, but takes the bound from a separate release so that the estimation noise is independent of $\lambda$.

\paragraph{Private set intersection.} Labeled PSI from homomorphic encryption~\cite{chen2017fast,chen2018labeled,cong2021labeled} builds on polynomial evaluation~\cite{freedman2004efficient} and cuckoo hashing~\cite{pagh2004cuckoo}. We hash identifiers with the 2HashDH oblivious PRF~\cite{jarecki2014round}, standardized in RFC~9497~\cite{rfc9497}. Circuit-PSI~\cite{rindal2021vole} would hide the match set from $\Rp$ as well (Remark~\ref{rem:modeM}).

\paragraph{Homomorphic encryption and differential privacy.} Li and Micciancio~\cite{li2021security} showed that decrypted CKKS values can leak the secret key, and Li, Micciancio, Schultz and Sorrell~\cite{li2022securing} use differential privacy to protect approximate HE. Our release floods the error statistically with noise that is also the DP noise of the statistic (Section~\ref{sec:privacy}).

The target is a ridge fit on the matched, column-joined data $\bX^I = [\dXR^{I} \mid \bXOI]$ with
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:gram}
  \bG := \bX^{I\top}\bX^I =
  \begin{bmatrix} \bA & \bC \\ \bC^{\top} & \bB \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
  \bc := \bX^{I\top}\by^{I} =
  \begin{bmatrix} \bcR \\ \bcO \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}

The two estimands, the ridge target $\btheta_\lambda = (\bG + \lambda\Psi)^{-1}\bc$ and the regression coefficient $\bbeta$, are defined in Section~\ref{sec:stats:estimands}.

\section{Threat model and guarantees}
\label{sec:defs}
% ==============================================================

Both parties are \emph{honest-but-curious}. They follow the protocol, including key generation and $\Rp$'s preparation of its inputs (clipping, the selection vector), and they try to learn what they can from their views. Channels are private and authenticated. $\Rp$ is the analyst and receives the output.

\begin{definition}[Datasets and adjacency]
\label{def:adj}
A dataset is $\Op$'s padded scope register $D = \{(\mathrm{id}_j, \bx_{O,j}, y_j)\}_{j \le \bar n_O}$, with the cohort $(\mathrm{id}, \bXR)$, the identifiers and $\Phi$ held fixed. $D \sim D'$ if they differ in $(\bx_O, y)$ of exactly one row, matched or not (\emph{replace-one} adjacency).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Zero-concentrated DP]
\label{def:dp}
A randomized mechanism $\mathcal M$ is $\rho$-zCDP if the R\'enyi divergence of order $a$ between $\mathcal M(D)$ and $\mathcal M(D')$ is at most $\rho a$ for all $a > 1$ and all $D \sim D'$~\cite{bun2016concentrated}. It is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP if $\Pr[\mathcal M(D) \in S] \le e^{\varepsilon}\Pr[\mathcal M(D') \in S] + \delta$ for all $D \sim D'$ and measurable $S$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[SIM-CDP and IND-CDP, after~\cite{mironov2009computational}]
\label{def:simcdp}
A protocol is \emph{$(\varepsilon, \delta)$-SIM-CDP for $\Rp$ with statistical slack $\gamma$} if there exist a mechanism $\mathcal M$ that is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP with respect to $\sim$ and a probabilistic polynomial-time simulator $\mathcal S$ such that, for every $D$ and every PPT distinguisher $\mathcal A$,
\[
  \bigl|\Pr[\mathcal A(\mathrm{view}_{\Rp}(D)) = 1] - \Pr[\mathcal A(\mathcal S(\mathcal M(D))) = 1]\bigr| \le \gamma + \mathrm{negl}(\lambda_{\mathrm{sec}}).
\]
It is \emph{$(\varepsilon, \delta)$-IND-CDP} if $\Pr[\mathcal A(\mathrm{view}_{\Rp}(D)) = 1] \le e^{\varepsilon}\Pr[\mathcal A(\mathrm{view}_{\Rp}(D')) = 1] + \delta + \mathrm{negl}(\lambda_{\mathrm{sec}})$ for all $D \sim D'$ and every PPT $\mathcal A$.
\end{definition}

Mironov et al.\ state these notions for pure $\varepsilon$; the extension to $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ is the standard one, and the slack $\gamma$ makes explicit the statistical terms that are not negligible in $\lambda_{\mathrm{sec}}$ at a concrete flooding parameter.

\begin{lemma}[From simulation to indistinguishability]
\label{lem:sim2ind}
$(\varepsilon, \delta)$-SIM-CDP with slack $\gamma$ implies $(\varepsilon, \delta + (1 + e^{\varepsilon})\gamma)$-IND-CDP.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Chain $\mathrm{view}(D) \approx \mathcal S(\mathcal M(D))$ (cost $\gamma$), the DP of $\mathcal S \circ \mathcal M$ (post-processing; factor $e^{\varepsilon}$, cost $\delta$), and $\mathcal S(\mathcal M(D')) \approx \mathrm{view}(D')$ (cost $e^{\varepsilon}\gamma$).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is revealed by design]
\label{rem:revealed}
$\Rp$ learns which of its cohort appear in $\Op$'s scope, and $n$. When membership is itself sensitive (e.g.\ a benefits register, where membership \emph{is} the outcome), this must be disclosed to data stewards. DP does not protect membership or $n$, nor $\Rp$'s own cohort in a published model, since $\bA$ is exact. A membership-private variant is sketched in Remark~\ref{rem:modeM}.
\end{remark}

\section{Building blocks}
\label{sec:blocks}
% ==============================================================

\subsection{Two-party threshold CKKS}
\label{sec:blocks:he}

CKKS~\cite{cheon2017homomorphic} encrypts vectors of $N/2$ real numbers. A ciphertext is a pair $(c_0, c_1)$ of polynomials modulo $q$, and decryption with the secret key $s$ gives
\[
  c_0 + c_1 s = \Ecd(\mathbf z) + e \pmod q,
\]
the encoded vector plus a small error. In the threshold variant the key is split additively, $s = s_R + s_O$, so decryption splits as $(c_0 + c_1 s_O) + c_1 s_R$: the first term needs $\Op$ and the second needs $\Rp$.

\paragraph{Key generation.} Following Mouchet et al.~\cite{mouchet2021multiparty}, from a common random polynomial $a$ each party draws its share and an error and publishes one polynomial,
\[
  p_{0,R} = -a s_R + e_R, \qquad p_{0,O} = -a s_O + e_O, \qquad \pk = \bigl(p_{0,R} + p_{0,O},\ a\bigr) = (-a s + e,\ a).
\]
Rotation keys for the slot shifts $2^i$ are generated the same way. The circuit has depth two and no ciphertext--ciphertext products, so no relinearization or conjugation key is needed.

\paragraph{Upload at half size.} $\Rp$ encrypts with its own share symmetrically, using a random $a$ expanded from a short seed: $c_0 = -a s_R + e + \Ecd(\mathbf v)$, $a = \mathrm{PRG}(\varsigma)$. It sends $(c_0, \varsigma)$. $\Op$ regenerates $a$ and sets $c_0 \gets c_0 - a s_O$, after which $c_0 + a s = \Ecd(\mathbf v) + e$: a ciphertext under the joint key.

\paragraph{What $\Op$ computes.} $\Op$ adds ciphertexts, multiplies them by its own plaintext columns, and rotates slots to sum a vector. None of this needs a key share.

\subsection{Oblivious PRF}
\label{sec:blocks:oprf}

$\Rp$ obtains $F_k(x) = H_2(x, H_1(x)^k)$ under $\Op$'s key $k$ without $\Op$ seeing $x$ and without $\Rp$ learning $k$ (2HashDH~\cite{jarecki2014round,rfc9497}). $\Rp$ sends $\alpha = H_1(x)^r$ for a random $r$; $\Op$ returns $\beta = \alpha^k$; $\Rp$ unblinds $\beta^{1/r} = H_1(x)^k$ and hashes. $\alpha$ is uniform in the group, so $\Op$ learns nothing about $x$. The output is split into an item part $F_k(x)$, used for matching, and a key part $F'_k(x)$, used to open labels. The OPRF makes every membership test an online interaction with $\Op$, which matters because identifiers such as social insurance numbers are a small, guessable space.

\subsection{Labeled PSI}
\label{sec:blocks:psi}

In labeled PSI~\cite{chen2017fast,chen2018labeled,cong2021labeled} the receiver learns which of its items are in the sender's set and, for each match, a label attached by the sender. $\Op$ hashes its items into bins, interpolates per bin a membership polynomial whose roots are the items and a label polynomial that maps each item to its encrypted label, and evaluates both on $\Rp$'s encrypted, cuckoo-hashed items. Here the label is the row's position $\tau(j)$ in $\Op$'s shuffled register. Appendix~\ref{app:psi} gives the details.

\subsection{zCDP and the discrete Gaussian}
\label{sec:blocks:dp}

A mechanism that adds Gaussian noise of s.d.\ $\sigma$ to a statistic with $\ell_2$ sensitivity $\Delta$ is exactly $\Delta^2/(2\sigma^2)$-zCDP, and so is the multivariate discrete Gaussian on $\Z^N$~\cite{bun2016concentrated,canonne2020discrete}. zCDP costs add under composition. A $\rho$-zCDP mechanism is $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP with $\varepsilon = \rho + 2\sqrt{\rho\ln(1/\delta)}$; the tight conversion of Canonne, Kamath and Steinke~\cite{canonne2020discrete} allows a larger $\rho$ at the same $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ (about $27\%$ larger at $\varepsilon = 1$, $\delta = 10^{-9}$). Floating-point samplers leak through their low-order bits~\cite{mironov2012significance}; we use an exact integer sampler.

\paragraph{Phase 0: setup (Algorithm~\ref{alg:setup}).} The parties agree on the parameter vector $\mathcal P$ (Section~\ref{sec:params}): the scope, the caps, the budget, the ledger cap of the base register, the statistical constants, the PSI parameters and the encoding scales. Each party preprocesses its own columns with public constants (Section~\ref{sec:protocol:preproc}), and the clipping bounds are chosen on the preprocessed public reference data or from domain knowledge. The sensitivities follow from the bounds alone (Section~\ref{sec:privacy:sens}). $\Op$ runs \textsc{Calibrate} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:sub}), which derives the flooding parameter, the CKKS scale and the noise from $\mathcal P$ and refuses the run if $\mathcal P$ is not admissible; $\Op$ also refuses if its ledger would exceed the cap (Section~\ref{sec:privacy:ledger}). $\Rp$ repeats \textsc{Calibrate}, whose outputs depend on public values only, and checks $\Op$'s. The parties run threshold key generation, and $\Op$ draws a fresh OPRF key, a fresh shuffle $\tau$, and prepares its PSI polynomials.

\tocheck{ChooseBounds: the evaluation uses the 99.5\% quantile of each block's norm on the preprocessed reference data. The optimized choice (quantile grid minimizing first-order MSE plus squared clipping bias) has not been specified or evaluated.}

\paragraph{Phase 1: link (Algorithm~\ref{alg:link}).} $\Rp$ pads its identifiers to $n_R^{\max}$, obtains their OPRF values, and runs labeled PSI. It learns $I$ and the positions $\pi(i) = \tau(j)$, and computes its own block
\[
  \bA = \sum_{i \in I} \bxd_i\bxd_i^\top
\]
exactly and locally. $\bA$ is never transmitted and never noised: it is a function of $(I, \bXR)$, both known to $\Rp$, and it does not change between adjacent datasets.

\paragraph{Phase 2: upload (Algorithm~\ref{alg:upload}).} $\Rp$ writes the selection vector $\bb$ and its features $\dXR$ over all $\bar n_O$ positions, in $\tau$-order and zero off the match, encrypts them under $s_R$ with seeded randomness, and sends them in chunks of at most $N/2$ slots. $\Op$ converts each ciphertext to the joint key.

\paragraph{Phase 3: aggregate (Algorithm~\ref{alg:aggregate}).} For each released entry $\Op$ multiplies one of $\Rp$'s encrypted vectors by one of its plaintext columns and sums:
\begin{align}
  \Enc{B_{jk}} &= \textstyle\sum_{t} \Enc{b_{t}}\,X_{O,tj}X_{O,tk}, &
  \Enc{C_{jk}} &= \textstyle\sum_{t} \Enc{\dot{X}_{R,tj}}\,X_{O,tk}, \label{eq:BC}\\
  \Enc{c_{O,k}} &= \textstyle\sum_{t} \Enc{b_{t}}\,X_{O,tk}\,y_{t}, &
  \Enc{c_{R,j}} &= \textstyle\sum_{t} \Enc{\dot{X}_{R,tj}}\,y_{t}, \label{eq:cOcR}
\end{align}
and $\Enc{\by^\top\by} = \sum_t \Enc{b_t}\,y_t^2$. Unmatched positions contribute zero to the values, because $\bb$ and $\dXR$ are zero there. $\Op$ then packs the results so that each released entry sits alone in its own slot: $M_1$ holds only $\triu\bB$ and $\bC$, for stage~1, and $M_2$ holds every stage-2 entry. A separate $M_y$ holds $\by^\top\by$ when it is not free (Section~\ref{sec:privacy:sens}).

\paragraph{Phase 4: release (Algorithm~\ref{alg:release}).} For each packed ciphertext $M = (c_0, c_1)$, $\Op$ re-randomizes it under the joint public key with fresh $(u, e_0, e_1)$, computes its noisy decryption share
\[
  h_O = c_0' + c_1' s_O + \eta, \qquad \eta \sim \DG(0, \sigma_c^2)^N \text{ fresh},
\]
charges its ledger, and sends $(c_1', h_O)$. It never sends $c_0'$. $\Rp$ computes $v = h_O + c_1' s_R = \Ecd(\mathbf z) + e + \eta$ and decodes. Stage~1 releases $M_1$ with s.d.\ $\sigma_1$, stage~2 releases $M_2$ with s.d.\ $\sigma_2$, and the releases use independent randomness.

\paragraph{Phase 5: solve and infer (Algorithm~\ref{alg:solve}).} From stage~1 and its exact $\bA$, $\Rp$ chooses the ridge $(\lambda, \Psi)$. After stage~2 it checks the gate $\hat\vartheta \ge 1$, solves $\tilde\btheta = (\hat\bG_2 + \lambda\Psi)^{-1}\hat\bc_2$, and computes both intervals (Section~\ref{sec:stats}). All of this is post-processing.

\begin{remark}[Changes relative to earlier drafts]
\label{rem:changes}
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, label=(\roman*)]
  \item \textbf{Keys.} $\Rp$ no longer holds the CKKS secret key. The key is shared, $\Op$ adds the noise inside its decryption share, and $c_0'$ is never transmitted, which closes a leak of $\Op$'s key share.
  \item \textbf{Flooding.} The DP noise $\eta$ itself floods every error term that depends on $\Op$'s data or secrets, statistically and in $\ell_2$. The earlier separately calibrated ``error channel'' and its cost $\rho_{\mathrm{err}}$ are gone.
  \item \textbf{Two releases.} The ridge is chosen from a first release instead of from a committed public eigenvalue bound, so it adapts to the data while staying independent of the estimation noise.
  \item \textbf{Accounting.} zCDP with the tight conversion replaces the analytic Gaussian calibration of a single release.
  \item \textbf{Linkage.} An OPRF with a fresh key per run replaces a keyed hash with a key known to $\Rp$, and OPRF queries are padded and counted against a quota.
  \item \textbf{Input checks.} The homomorphic input checks of v3 are removed: they addressed only a malicious researcher, and one could be bypassed. Robustness to a deviating researcher is an extension (Section~\ref{sec:ext}).
  \item \textbf{Bias correction.} The zero-budget correction of Section~\ref{sec:bias} is now an option, off in our evaluation.
  \item \textbf{Preprocessing.} Each party transforms its own columns in Setup, with public constants (Section~\ref{sec:protocol:preproc}).
  \item \textbf{Parameters.} The protocol is stated for a parameter vector with explicit admissibility constraints (Section~\ref{sec:params}). The flooding parameter is derived from the budget and the ledger cap, the PSI replies are sanitized with their own flooding parameter, and the ledger has a specified composition rule.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\subsection{Per-party preprocessing}
\label{sec:protocol:preproc}

The noise is sized to the clipping bounds, so every record whose norm is well below its bound wastes part of the budget. After standardization, real features are often heavy-tailed. On the protein dataset of Section~\ref{sec:eval:e2e}, the $99.5\%$ quantile of the squared norm of $\Rp$'s block (with the intercept) is $9.5$ times its median, and that of $\Op$'s block $19$ times. Bounds at these quantiles leave typical records using a small part of the budget. Setup therefore includes a preprocessing step, \textsc{Preprocess} in Algorithm~\ref{alg:sub}, in which each party transforms its own columns:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, label=(\roman*)]
  \item standardize each column with public constants, from public reference data or domain knowledge;
  \item optionally, a per-column tail transform with public constants: winsorizing at $|z| \le c$, a rank-Gaussian transform from public reference quantiles, or a log transform of a skewed variable;
  \item optionally, \emph{block normalization}: each party scales its own block of every row to unit norm ($\Rp$ excludes the intercept, so $\|\bxd\|^2 = 2$; $\Op$ gets $\|\bx_O\| = 1$). A clipped log of the block norm may be appended as an extra feature to keep the magnitude.
\end{enumerate}
$\Op$ standardizes $y$, and may winsorize it, in the same way. The bounds are then chosen on the transformed reference data. For block normalization they are exact: $B_R^2 = 2$, $B_O = 1$.

\begin{proposition}[Preprocessing preserves the guarantees]
\label{prop:preproc}
Let each party's transform be a fixed function of its own record and of public constants. Then the protocol run on the transformed data, with bounds that hold on the transformed domain, has the guarantees of Table~\ref{tab:guarantees} with respect to replace-one adjacency on the raw data.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\Op$'s transform maps one raw record to one transformed record, so changing one raw record $(\bx_O, y)$ changes exactly one transformed record, which still satisfies the bounds; $\Rp$'s records are fixed under $\sim$. The sensitivity of Section~\ref{sec:privacy:sens} therefore bounds the change, and the rest of the analysis is unchanged. The constants are data-independent, as \textsc{Calibrate}'s outputs must be. $\Rp$ may standardize with its own constants, since its data are not protected against itself, but it commits to $B_R$.
\end{proof}

The estimand is the regression on the transformed features. Winsorizing and log transforms keep the coefficients interpretable. Block normalization changes their meaning: each block contributes through its direction only, unless the log-norm feature is appended. It is therefore the choice for prediction rather than for interpreting coefficients.

\paragraph{Why not joint row normalization.} Wang's preprocessing for AdaSSP normalizes each whole row $(\bx_R, \bx_O)$, which needs both parties' columns. We considered two ways to do this here.
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \emph{Exact joint clipping} computes the scale factor $\min\{1, B/\|\bx\|\}$ under encryption from $\Rp$'s encrypted row norms and $\Op$'s plaintext ones. This needs a high-degree polynomial approximation and ciphertext--ciphertext products. Worse, scaling $\Rp$'s part by a factor that depends on $\Op$'s data makes $\bA$ depend on $\Op$'s data, so $\bA$ could no longer be exact.
  \item \emph{Clipping only $\Op$'s part to the remaining budget} $B^2 - \|\bxd\|^2$ keeps $\bA$ exact and the circuit linear. $\Rp$ assigns each matched record to one of $K$ norm classes and uploads its selection vector and features split by class, still encrypted, and $\Op$ pre-clips its columns once per class in plaintext, at about $K$ times the upload and aggregation cost. On protein it does not pay. A joint budget alone raises $\Delta$ from $87.3$ to $102$, because the sensitivity is dominated by $\Op$'s own norm, which enters twice, and records with a small $\Rp$-norm may then use more of it. Keeping $\Op$'s separate cap as well lowers $\Delta$ only to $82$, about $12\%$ fewer records.
\end{itemize}
The gap is caused by heavy tails under loose bounds, not by the lack of a joint bound, and per-party transforms close most of it. In end-to-end runs on protein, block normalization brings private test error within $2$--$6\%$ of non-private, against $12$--$21\%$ with standardization alone (Table~\ref{tab:e2e_protein}).

\subsection{Sensitivity under replace-one adjacency}
\label{sec:privacy:sens}

Stage~2 releases the stacked statistic
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf z_2(D) = \bigl(\triu(\bB),\; \bC,\; \bcR,\; \bcO,\; \by^{\top}\by\bigr),
  \label{eq:release}
\end{equation}
without $\by^\top\by$ in separate mode, and stage~1 releases $\mathbf z_1(D) = (\triu\bB, \bC)$. An unmatched row contributes nothing to these values. Replacing one matched row's $(\bx, y)$ by $(\bx', y')$, with $\Rp$'s row $\bxd$ fixed, changes $\mathbf z_2$ by
\[
  \bigl(\triu(\bx\bx^{\top} - \bx'\bx'^{\top}),\;
  \bxd(\bx - \bx')^{\top},\;
  \bxd(y - y'),\;
  \bx y - \bx'y',\;
  y^{2} - y'^{2}\bigr).
\]
Every entry of a release gets noise of the same s.d., so the relevant sensitivity is the $\ell_2$ norm of this change, maximized over the bounds.

\begin{proposition}[Replace-one sensitivity]
\label{prop:sens}
With $r = B_R^2$, $o = B_O^2$, $w = B_y^2$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{add}}^2 = (o + r)(o + w) + w^2$ the add/remove bound, $\Delta_{\mathrm{add}} \le \Delta \le 2\Delta_{\mathrm{add}}$, and
\begin{equation}
  \Delta^{2} =
  \begin{cases}
    \tfrac12(2o + r + w)^{2} + 2rw & \text{if } |r - w| \le 2o,\\[2pt]
    4r(o + w) & \text{if } r - w \ge 2o,\\[2pt]
    \max_{t \in [-B_{y}, B_{y}]} F\bigl(c^{\ast}(t), t\bigr) & \text{if } w - r > 2o,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:sens_closed}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align*}
  F(c,t) &= 2o^{2}(1 - c^{2}) + 2ro(1 - c) + r(B_{y} - t)^{2} + o(w + t^{2}) - 2ocB_{y}t + (w - t^{2})^{2},\\
  c^{\ast}(t) &= \min\bigl\{1, \max\{-1, -(r + B_{y}t)/(2o)\}\bigr\}.
\end{align*}
For stage~1, $\Delta_G = (2o + r)/\sqrt2$ if $r \le 2o$ and $2\sqrt{ro}$ otherwise. These are exact suprema for $d_O \ge 2$ and valid, conservative bounds for $d_O = 1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The lower bound replaces $(\bx, y)$ with $(\mathbf 0, 0)$, the upper bound passes through that dataset with the triangle inequality. At the optimum $\|\bxd\| = B_R$ and $\|\bx\| = \|\bx'\| = B_O$; the remaining dependence is on $c = \cos\angle(\bx, \bx')$ and $t = y'$, and maximizing the concave quadratic in $c$ gives $c^\ast(t)$. In regime~1 the worst case is $y' = -y$ with $c = -(r - w)/(2o)$ and a diagonal change in $\bB$; in regime~2 it is $(\bx, y) \mapsto (-\bx, -y)$. Regime~3 reduces to the roots of three cubics. The full proof is in the companion proofs document (Part~B, Theorem~S1).
\end{proof}

\tocheck{DeltaRep in regime 3: the specification says one-dimensional maximization rounded up; the proofs give an exact enumeration over the roots of three cubics. Adopt the exact enumeration (keep rounding up as a safeguard)?}

\begin{remark}[When $\by^\top\by$ is free]
\label{rem:yyfree}
Releasing $\by^\top\by$ costs nothing exactly when $\Delta = \Delta_{\neg yy}$. This holds whenever $w \le 2(r + o)$ and never when $w > 3(r + o)$; in between it is decided numerically. It is false in general: for $(B_R, B_O, B_y) = (1, 1, 5)$, $\Delta = 26.07$ with $\by^\top\by$ against $14.14$ without. Algorithm~\ref{alg:setup} therefore releases $\by^\top\by$ in stage~2 only when it is free, and otherwise in its own release with sensitivity $\Delta_y = w$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Upper triangle or full matrix]
\label{rem:triu}
For $d_O \ge 2$ the supremum is the same whether the change in $\bB$ is measured on its upper triangle or by the full Frobenius norm: the $\triu$ maximum is attained only when the change is diagonal. Releasing both triangles of $\bB$ with independent noise would therefore cost the same worst-case $\rho_2$, and averaging the two copies would halve the variance of the off-diagonal entries.
\end{remark}

\tocheck{Design choice raised by the remark above: release the full matrix B (both triangles, independent noise) instead of its upper triangle? Same worst-case privacy cost, lower variance off the diagonal; the bias formulas and the bootstrap would need the corresponding noise covariance.}

\subsection{The release mechanism}
\label{sec:privacy:mech}

For release $s$, let $\mathbf z_s(D) \in \R^{N_s}$ hold the released entries in their slots and zeros elsewhere.

\begin{lemma}[Sensitivity of the encoded statistic]
\label{lem:enc}
$\|\tau_{\mathrm{can}}^{-1}(\mathbf z)\|_2 = \sqrt{2/N}\,\|\mathbf z\|_2$. Hence $\sup_{D \sim D'} \|\Ecd_{\Omega}(\mathbf z_s(D)) - \Ecd_{\Omega}(\mathbf z_s(D'))\|_2 \le \sqrt{2/N}\,\Omega\,\Delta_s + \sqrt N$, where $\sqrt N$ bounds the difference of the two coordinate-wise roundings.
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{The centre.} The decrypted value differs from $\Ecd_{\Omega_f}(\mathbf z_s(D))$ also by deterministic, data-dependent terms, chiefly the spill of the one-hot masks. These terms are part of the query, not hints about secrets, so the mechanism is centred at the noiseless output of the circuit,
\[
  \hat{\mathbf z}_s(D) = \sum_{e \in \mathcal E_s}\frac{1}{q_1q_2}\,\mathrm{Tr}\Bigl(\sum_{h \le m} m_{U_e,h}\,m_{V_e,h}\Bigr)m_{u_e}, \qquad
  f_s(D) = \lfloor \hat{\mathbf z}_s(D) \rceil \in \Z^N,
\]
where $m_{U_e,h}$, $m_{V_e,h}$ and $m_{u_e}$ are the encodings of $\Rp$'s vectors at $\Omega_U$, of $\Op$'s vectors at $\Omega_V$ and of the mask at $\Omega_u$, and $\mathrm{Tr}$ is the trace that \textsc{EvalSum} computes. $f_s(D)$ depends on the data and the public parameters only, not on any key, error or coin. Its sensitivity is (proofs, Part~A, corollary on the sensitivity of the centre)
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{D \sim D'}\|f_s(D) - f_s(D')\|_2 \le \widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{enc},s} := \sqrt{2/N}\,\Omega_f\Delta_s + \Delta_{x,s} + \sqrt N,
  \label{eq:denc}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_{x,s}$, an explicit function of $N$, the encoding scales, $q_1$, $q_2$, $|\mathcal E_s|$, the bounds and $\Delta_s$, bounds the change of the rounding and spill terms. $\Omega_f$ is the exact final scale, tracked from the primes and the encoding scales and never a nominal power of two, and $\Rp$ decodes with it. At the evaluation settings $\Delta_{x,2} \approx 2^{27.6}$ against $\sqrt{2/N}\,\Omega_f\Delta_2 \approx 2^{62.7}$, so centring changes $\rho_s$ by a relative $2^{-34}$. Flooding the mask spill instead would need $\log_2\Omega_f$ of about $67$--$71$ rather than $61.5$ at the proofs' example ($\bar n_O = 10^6$, $\varepsilon = 1$).

\paragraph{Error decomposition.} The value $\Rp$ reconstructs in a release is
\[
  v_s = h_O + c_1' s_R = c_0' + c_1' s + \eta_s = f_s(D) + e_{\mathrm{tot},s} + \eta_s \pmod{q_0}.
\]
$e_{\mathrm{tot},s} \in \Z^N$ collects every term that depends on a key, an error or a coin: $\Rp$'s encryption errors multiplied by $\Op$'s data, the rescaling and ModDown roundings, the key-switching error (which involves the errors of $\Op$'s key shares), the mask spill of these errors, the rounding of the centre, and the fresh-encryption error $e\,u + e_0 + e_1 s$ of the re-randomization.

\begin{lemma}[Smudging]
\label{lem:smudge}
For $e \in \Z^N$ and $\eta \sim \mathcal D_{\Z^N, \sigma_c}$, $\mathrm{SD}(\eta + e, \eta) \le \|e\|_2/(2\sigma_c)$, and, for $\sigma_c$ above the smoothing parameter, $\mathrm{SD}(\eta + e, \eta) \le \|e\|_2/(\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_c) + \mathrm{negl}$. If $\sigma_c \ge \max\{2^{\kappa}B_{\mathrm{tot}}/\sqrt{2\pi},\ 2^{20}\}$ and $\|e\|_2 \le B_{\mathrm{tot}}$, the distance is at most $2^{-\kappa}(1 + 2^{-80}) + 2^{-97}$, also given any side information independent of $\eta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Worst-case error bound]
\label{thm:btot}
For the circuit of Algorithm~\ref{alg:aggregate} there is an explicit bound $B_{\mathrm{tot},s}$, a function of public parameters only ($N$, $\bar n_O$, $d_R$, $d_O$, $B_R$, $B_O$, $B_y$, the primes and the key-switching gadget, $\Omega_U$, $\Omega_V$, $\Omega_u$, $\delta_B$ and the error distribution), such that $\Pr[\|e_{\mathrm{tot},s}\|_2 \le B_{\mathrm{tot},s}] \ge 1 - \delta_B$ for every dataset in the clipped domain, the probability being over keys and fresh randomness only (proofs, Part~A, Theorem~O1).
\end{theorem}

At the proofs' example ($N = 2^{14}$, $\bar n_O = 10^6$, the bounds of the simulation population, all scales and both rescaling primes $2^{52.5}$, centred key switching, $\delta_B = 2^{-50}$) $B_{\mathrm{tot},2} \approx 2^{25.3}$, and the bound was checked against OpenFHE's noise estimates with a margin of $6$--$8$ bits. With OpenFHE's key switching, whose fast base conversions are not centred, and the evaluation settings of Section~\ref{sec:params}, $B_{\mathrm{tot},2} \approx 2^{26.6}$.

\textsc{Calibrate} must use this worst-case, data-independent bound. A bound estimated from the actual ciphertexts would depend on the data, leak through $\sigma_{c,s}$, and not be a worst-case bound.

\begin{theorem}[Output privacy against an honest-but-curious $\Rp$]
\label{thm:output}
Let the parameter vector satisfy the constraints of Section~\ref{sec:params}, and let \textup{\textsc{Calibrate}}'s outputs, which depend on public values only, satisfy for every release $s$
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, label=(\roman*)]
  \item $\sigma_{c,s} \ge \max\{2^{\kappa}B_{\mathrm{tot},s}/\sqrt{2\pi},\ 2^{20}\}$, with $B_{\mathrm{tot},s}$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:btot};
  \item $\rho_s = \widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{enc},s}^2/(2\sigma_{c,s}^2)$ and $\sum_s\rho_s \le \rho_{\mathrm{run}}$, where $\rho_{\mathrm{run}}$ is the largest $\rho$ whose tight conversion gives $(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}}, \delta_{\mathrm{DP}})$, with
  \[
    \gamma = n_{\mathrm{rel}}\bigl(\delta_B + 2^{-\kappa}(1 + 2^{-80}) + 2^{-97}\bigr), \qquad \delta_{\mathrm{DP}} = \delta_{\mathrm{run}} - (1 + e^{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}}})\gamma > 0 .
  \]
\end{enumerate}
Assume RLWE for the re-randomizer (one sample per release, modulus $q_0$) and for $\Op$'s key shares (multi-sample, with circular security for the rotation-key shares), at ring dimension $N$ and modulus $QP$ with $\log_2(QP) \le L_{\max}(N, \lambda_{\mathrm{sec}})$. Then $\Rp$'s view of one run is $(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}}, \delta_{\mathrm{DP}})$-SIM-CDP for $\sim$ with statistical slack $\gamma$, and therefore $(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}}, \delta_{\mathrm{run}})$-IND-CDP. The ideal mechanism $\mathcal M(D) = (f_s(D) + \eta_s)_s$ is a composition of lattice discrete Gaussian mechanisms and is $\rho_{\mathrm{run}}$-zCDP. PSI security is not needed for this guarantee: the identifiers are fixed under $\sim$, so the PSI transcript does not depend on $(\bx_O, y)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
A sequence of hybrids. H0 is the real view: setup messages, PSI messages, and $\{(c'_{1,s}, h_{O,s})\}_s$. H1 rewrites $h_{O,s} = v_s - c'_{1,s}s_R$, which is identical. H2 replaces $v_s$ by $f_s(D) + \eta_s$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:smudge} with side information and Theorem~\ref{thm:btot} this costs at most $\delta_B + 2^{-\kappa}(1 + 2^{-80}) + 2^{-97}$ per release, $\gamma$ in total. After H2, $(u_s, e_{1,s})$ appear only through $c'_{1,s} = c_{1,s} + a u_s + e_{1,s}$, because $c'_{0,s}$ is never sent. H3 replaces $c'_{1,s}$ by a uniform element (RLWE with secret $u_s$; fresh $u_s$ per release). H4 simulates the setup messages from the RLWE assumption on $\Op$'s key shares. The simulator, given $\mathcal M(D)$ and $s_R$, samples a uniform $c_1^\ast$ per release and outputs $h^\ast = \mathcal M(D)_s - c_1^\ast s_R$. The multivariate discrete Gaussian with sensitivity $\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{enc},s}$ is exactly $\rho_s$-zCDP~\cite{canonne2020discrete}; costs add, and the tight conversion gives $(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}}, \delta_{\mathrm{DP}})$-DP. Lemma~\ref{lem:sim2ind} turns the slack into $\delta_{\mathrm{run}} = \delta_{\mathrm{DP}} + (1 + e^{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}}})\gamma$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the release format matters]
\label{rem:format}
If $\Op$ sent $c_0'$, or a share $c_1' s_O$ without $\eta$, $\Rp$ would learn $c_1' s_O$ exactly and could recover $s_O$, since $c_1'$ is invertible with overwhelming probability. Re-randomization matters too. The evaluated $c_1$ is computed from public values and $\Op$'s plaintext columns, and $\Rp$ chose the seed of $a$, so without it $c_1$ could reveal $\Op$'s data. In a real OpenFHE run of an earlier specification that added the noise as a plaintext scalar, without re-randomizing, $\Rp$ identified the true dataset in 20 of 20 trials, for a matched and for an unmatched record, where an $\varepsilon = 1$ guarantee caps the success rate at $e/(1 + e) \approx 73\%$. Reusing $\eta$ or $u$ across releases would reveal $v_2 - v_1$ exactly, so both are fresh every time. Stage~1 uses its own packed ciphertext $M_1$, which depends only on $\triu\bB$ and $\bC$, so its error cannot depend on entries released only in stage~2.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Calibration}
\label{sec:privacy:calib}

\textsc{Calibrate} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:sub}) derives every quantity the release needs from the parameter vector, using public values only, in five steps.
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, label=(\arabic*)]
  \item \emph{Mode and sensitivities.} $\Delta$, $\Delta_{\neg yy}$, $\Delta_G$ and $\Delta_y$ from the bounds (Proposition~\ref{prop:sens}). $\by^\top\by$ joins stage~2 iff it is free (Remark~\ref{rem:yyfree}), so the number of releases is $n_{\mathrm{rel}} = 2$ (joint mode) or $3$ (separate mode).
  \item \emph{Flooding parameter and slack.} $\kappa$ from~\eqref{eq:kappa} below, unless the parameters fix it; $\gamma = n_{\mathrm{rel}}(\delta_B + 2^{-\kappa}(1 + 2^{-80}) + 2^{-97})$ and $\delta_{\mathrm{DP}} = \delta_{\mathrm{run}} - (1 + e^{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}}})\gamma$.
  \item \emph{Budget.} $\rho_{\mathrm{run}}$, the largest $\rho$ whose tight conversion gives $(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}}, \delta_{\mathrm{DP}})$, split as $\rho_1 = f_1\rho_{\mathrm{run}}$, $\rho_y = f_y\rho_{\mathrm{run}}$ in separate mode, and $\rho_2$ the rest.
  \item \emph{Scale and noise.} The smallest admissible final scale $\Omega_f$ (that is, the smallest $\Omega_V$, given $\Omega_U$ and $\Omega_u$) at which $\sigma_{c,s} \ge \max\{2^{\kappa}B_{\mathrm{tot},s}(\Omega_f)/\sqrt{2\pi},\ 2^{20}\}$ for every release, with $\sigma_{c,s}^2 = \lceil \widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{enc},s}^2/(2\rho_s) \rceil$ an integer, as the exact sampler needs. Rounding up only lowers the cost below $\rho_s$.
  \item \emph{Modulus and security.} $q_0 > 2(\Omega_f S_{\max} + 2^{\kappa}B_{\mathrm{tot},\max} + t_\eta\,\sigma_{c,\max})$ with $S_{\max} = \bar n_O\max(r, o, w, \sqrt{ro}, \sqrt{rw}, \sqrt{ow})$, so that honest inputs never wrap around, and $\log_2(QP) \le L_{\max}(N, \lambda_{\mathrm{sec}})$, the largest modulus at which RLWE with ring dimension $N$ and ternary secrets has security $\lambda_{\mathrm{sec}}$~\cite{albrecht2018he}.
\end{enumerate}
\textsc{Calibrate} refuses the run when a step has no solution: no admissible $\kappa$, $\delta_{\mathrm{DP}} \le 0$, no scale that floods, $q_0$ too small, or a chain above the security bound.

\paragraph{The noise.} The induced slot-domain noise is, up to a relative term $(\Delta_{x,s} + \sqrt N)\sqrt{N/2}/(\Omega_f\Delta_s)$ that is below $10^{-10}$ at the evaluation settings,
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_1 = \frac{\Delta_G}{\sqrt{2 f_1\rho_{\mathrm{run}}}}, \qquad
  \sigma_2 = \frac{\Delta}{\sqrt{2(1 - f_1)\rho_{\mathrm{run}}}}, \qquad
  \sqrt{\rho_{\mathrm{run}}} \gtrsim \sqrt{L + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}}} - \sqrt L, \quad L = \ln(1/\delta_{\mathrm{DP}}),
  \label{eq:sigmas}
\end{equation}
in joint mode ($\Delta_{\neg yy}$ and $1 - f_1 - f_y$ replace $\Delta$ and $1 - f_1$ in separate mode). The last inequality, from the simple conversion, gives a planning formula; over $\varepsilon \in [0.1, 8]$ and $\delta \in [10^{-9}, 10^{-6}]$ it is conservative by $6$--$33\%$ in $\sigma_2$ (Section~\ref{sec:eval:calib}).

\paragraph{The flooding parameter.} The same $\eta$ is the DP noise and floods $e_{\mathrm{tot},s}$, and the flooding enters the guarantee only through the slack $\gamma$, which Lemma~\ref{lem:sim2ind} converts with the factor $1 + e^{\varepsilon}$. The flooding parameter is therefore a function of the budget, not a constant. Unless the parameters fix it, \textsc{Calibrate} sets
\begin{equation}
  \kappa = \max\{\kappa_{\min},\ \kappa_{\mathrm{run}},\ \kappa_L\},
  \label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_{\mathrm{run}}$ is the smallest integer with $(1 + e^{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}}})\gamma \le \zeta\,\delta_{\mathrm{run}}$, and $\kappa_L$ is the smallest integer with $\gamma \le (\tilde\rho_{\mathrm{run}}/\rho_L)\,\Gamma_L$, this run's share of the ledger's slack capacity (Section~\ref{sec:privacy:ledger}); $\tilde\rho_{\mathrm{run}}$ is the budget at $\delta_{\mathrm{DP}} = (1 - \zeta)\delta_{\mathrm{run}}$, a lower bound on $\rho_{\mathrm{run}}$ once $\kappa \ge \kappa_{\mathrm{run}}$. The three terms have separate jobs.
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item $\kappa_{\mathrm{run}}$ makes the per-run statement hold with the slack taking at most a share $\zeta$ of $\delta_{\mathrm{run}}$. $\gamma$ falls with $\kappa$ to the floor $n_{\mathrm{rel}}(\delta_B + 2^{-97})$, so $\kappa_{\mathrm{run}}$ exists iff $n_{\mathrm{rel}}(1 + e^{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}}})(\delta_B + 2^{-97}) < \zeta\delta_{\mathrm{run}}$; otherwise $\delta_B$ must be lowered.
  \item $\kappa_L$ makes the ledger run out of zCDP budget before it runs out of slack, whatever the order and the sizes of the runs.
  \item $\kappa_{\min}$ is a statistical security floor. $\gamma$ also bounds what $\Rp$ can learn beyond $\mathcal M(D)$, including about $\Op$'s key share, so it should not grow with a generous $\delta_{\mathrm{run}}$.
\end{itemize}
$\zeta$ trades budget for bits. Shrinking $\delta_{\mathrm{DP}}$ from $\delta_{\mathrm{run}}$ to $(1 - \zeta)\delta_{\mathrm{run}}$ lowers $\rho_{\mathrm{run}}$ by a relative $\zeta/\sqrt{L(L + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}})}$ to first order (simple conversion, $L = \ln(1/\delta_{\mathrm{run}})$), so $\sigma_2$ rises by half that, while each unit of $\kappa$ adds one bit to $\Omega_f$ and to $q_0$. At $\delta_{\mathrm{run}} = 10^{-9}$ and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}} \in \{1, 4, 8\}$ the tight conversion loses $0.46$--$0.59\%$ of $\rho_{\mathrm{run}}$ at $\zeta = 0.1$ and $0.04$--$0.06\%$ at $\zeta = 0.01$, and $\zeta = 0.01$ raises $\kappa$ by $3$ bits at $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}} = 4$ and $5$ at $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}} = 8$ (by at most $1$ where the floor $\kappa_{\min} = 40$ binds). Table~\ref{tab:kappa} gives $\kappa$ and the resulting scale at the evaluation settings.

\paragraph{The scale.} Since $\sigma_{c,s} = \sigma_s\Omega_f\sqrt{2/N}$ and $\sigma_s$ is fixed by the budget, $\Op$ meets the flooding condition by raising $\Omega_f$; stage~2 binds:
\[
  \log_2\Omega_f \approx \kappa + \log_2 B_{\mathrm{tot},2} + \log_2\sqrt{N/2} - \log_2\sqrt{2\pi} - \log_2\sigma_2 .
\]
Example: for the simulation population at $\varepsilon = 1$, $\delta = 10^{-9}$, $N = 2^{14}$ and $\kappa = 40$, the proofs' bound gives about $40 + 25.3 + 6.5 - 1.3 - 9.0 \approx 61.5$, and the bound for OpenFHE's key switching $62.75$ (Table~\ref{tab:kappa}).

\paragraph{Encoding scales.} We use the circuit as written (Algorithm~\ref{alg:aggregate}) with separate encoding scales: $\Omega_U$ and $\Omega_u$ are parameters, \textsc{Calibrate} chooses $\Omega_V$, and $\Omega_f = \Omega_U\Omega_V\Omega_u/(q_1q_2)$ follows and is tracked exactly. The final scale is therefore not tied to the size of a rescaling prime, which a 64-bit implementation keeps below $2^{60}$. The constraint on $\Omega_U$ comes from $B_{\mathrm{tot}}$: the term from $\Rp$'s encryption errors grows like $(\Omega_f/\Omega_U)\sqrt{\bar n_O}$. With $\Omega_U \ge \Omega_f$ it stays below the other terms at every register size; with $\Omega_U < \Omega_f$ the required scale grows with $\bar n_O$, and for large enough $\kappa$ no scale floods, because $B_{\mathrm{tot}}$ then grows as fast as $\Omega_f$, and \textsc{Calibrate} refuses. In the evaluation $\Omega_U = 2^{68}$ and $\Omega_u = q_1$. The required scale is then independent of $\bar n_O \in [4096, 10^6]$ to within $0.05$ bits for $\varepsilon \le 4$, and grows by $0.4$ bits at $\varepsilon = 8$, where $\Omega_f > \Omega_U$ (Table~\ref{tab:kappa}). With $\Omega_U = q_2 \approx 2^{59}$ instead, the required scale reaches $2^{70.3}$ at $\bar n_O = 10^6$, $\varepsilon = 4$ and $\kappa = 40$. The proof review's restructured circuit, which needs about $2^{53.7}$, is not used: its proof is incomplete, and with decoupled scales the larger scale costs nothing.

\subsection{The ledger and composition across runs}
\label{sec:privacy:ledger}

$\Op$ keeps one ledger per base register, shared across all researchers and scopes, and refuses to produce a decryption share once the cap would be exceeded. Because no decryption is possible without $\Op$'s share, the cap is enforced technically, not by policy. A refused stage-2 gate (Section~\ref{sec:stats:stage2}) still spends the budget. The OPRF quota limits membership look-ups across sessions as a matter of policy.

\paragraph{Parameters and state.} The ledger's parameters are a cap $(\varepsilon_L, \delta_L)$ and the share $\zeta_L \in (0, 1)$ of $\delta_L$ reserved for statistical slack. From them, $\rho_L$ is the largest $\rho$ whose tight conversion gives $(\varepsilon_L, (1 - \zeta_L)\delta_L)$, and $\Gamma_L = \zeta_L\delta_L/(1 + e^{\varepsilon_L})$. The state is the sum $\rho_\Sigma$ of the zCDP costs $\rho_s$ and the sum $\Gamma_\Sigma$ of the raw slacks $\gamma_s = \delta_B + 2^{-\kappa}(1 + 2^{-80}) + 2^{-97}$ of every release made on the register. The raw slacks add, not the converted ones $(1 + e^{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}}})\gamma$: they are converted once, at the cap's $\varepsilon_L$.

\paragraph{Admission.} Write $\delta(\rho, \varepsilon)$ for the tight conversion.
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \emph{Runs fixed in advance} (non-adaptive). Admit a run iff $\delta(\rho_\Sigma + \rho_{\mathrm{run}}, \varepsilon_L) + (1 + e^{\varepsilon_L})(\Gamma_\Sigma + \gamma) \le \delta_L$.
  \item \emph{Runs chosen after seeing earlier results} (adaptive). Admit a run iff $\rho_\Sigma + \rho_{\mathrm{run}} \le \rho_L$ and $\Gamma_\Sigma + \gamma \le \Gamma_L$. This is a privacy filter in the sense of Rogers et al.~\cite{rogers2016odometers}, and it is the rule for a shared register, where later runs are rarely fixed in advance. With $\kappa_L$ in~\eqref{eq:kappa} the slack condition holds whenever the zCDP condition does.
\end{itemize}
A register on which a single run is allowed is the special case $(\varepsilon_L, \delta_L) = (\varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}}, \delta_{\mathrm{run}})$ under the first rule, which that run fills exactly; then $\kappa_L = \kappa_{\mathrm{run}}$.

\begin{proposition}[Composition across runs]
\label{prop:compose}
Let runs $k = 1, 2, \dots$ on one base register use independent keys, shuffles and randomness, let each satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:output} with zCDP cost $\rho_k$ and slack $\gamma_k$, and let the number of runs be polynomial in $\lambda_{\mathrm{sec}}$. If the runs are fixed in advance and admitted by the first rule, or chosen adaptively and admitted by the second, then the joint view of all researchers is $(\varepsilon_L, \delta_L)$-IND-CDP for $\sim$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
\emph{Simulation, run by run.} Replace the runs one at a time by their simulations $\mathcal S_k(\mathcal M_k(D))$. Run $k$ uses keys and randomness independent of the other runs, so the reduction for each hybrid step can generate every other run, real or simulated, from $D$. This holds also when the parameters of run $k$ are a function of the researchers' views of earlier runs, since the reduction can compute that function. Step $k$ costs the expectation of $\gamma_k$ plus a negligible term, so the joint view is simulated from $(\mathcal M_k(D))_k$ with slack $\Gamma = \E\sum_k\gamma_k$, which is at most $\Gamma_\Sigma$ for fixed runs and at most $\Gamma_L$ under the filter. For fixed runs this is the composition lemma of the proofs (Part~A); the adaptive case is the same hybrid.
\emph{The ideal mechanisms.} For fixed runs, zCDP costs add~\cite{bun2016concentrated}. For adaptive runs, $\mathcal M_k$ is $\rho_k$-zCDP, that is, $(a, a\rho_k)$-R\'enyi DP for every order $a > 1$, with $\rho_k$ a function of earlier outputs and $\sum_k\rho_k \le \rho_L$ always. The R\'enyi filter of Feldman and Zrnic~\cite{feldman2021individual} (Theorem~1.1), applied at each order $a$, makes the composition $(a, a\rho_L)$-R\'enyi DP for every $a$, that is, $\rho_L$-zCDP.
\emph{Conversion.} The tight conversion gives $(\varepsilon_L, \delta(\rho, \varepsilon_L))$-DP for the composed mechanism, and Lemma~\ref{lem:sim2ind} adds $(1 + e^{\varepsilon_L})\Gamma$; each admission rule keeps the sum within $\delta_L$.
\end{proof}

\tocheck{Composition: the adaptive hybrid of Proposition~\ref{prop:compose} is argued here but not yet written out in the proofs, which prove the non-adaptive case. The literature was checked only through search-engine extracts, not the full texts: Feldman and Zrnic's Theorem~1.1 as quoted from the arXiv version (check its number in the NeurIPS version, and the page numbers); the pages of Rogers et al.\ (1921--1929); the volume and pages of Whitehouse et al.\ (PMLR~202, 36990--37007).}

Two consequences. First, a statement over many runs converts the slack with $1 + e^{\varepsilon_L}$, not $1 + e^{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}}}$, so a ledger with a large $\varepsilon_L$ needs a larger $\kappa$ per run. At $(\varepsilon_L, \delta_L, \zeta_L) = (10, 10^{-6}, 0.1)$, for example, $\kappa_L$ is $48$, $46$, $44$, $42$ and $40$ for $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{run}} = 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8$ (Table~\ref{tab:kappa}); at $\varepsilon_L = 20$ the floor $n_{\mathrm{rel}}\delta_B$ alone exceeds every run's share unless $\delta_B$ is lowered well below $2^{-50}$. Second, the filter loses nothing against non-adaptive composition, because zCDP composes by summing a single parameter. For $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP, filters that match advanced composition, and a filter for approximate zCDP, are given by Whitehouse et al.~\cite{whitehouse2023fully}.

\begin{remark}[A sharper slack]
\label{rem:sharperslack}
The factor $1 + e^{\varepsilon_L}$ comes from measuring the smudging step of Theorem~\ref{thm:output} in statistical distance. A shifted discrete Gaussian $\eta + e$ with $\|e\|_2 \le B_{\mathrm{tot}}$ is also $(\varepsilon', \delta')$-indistinguishable from $\eta$ with $\varepsilon'$ of order $2^{-\kappa}\sqrt{\ln(1/\delta')}$. Measuring that step in hockey-stick divergence would move most of the slack into a negligible $\varepsilon'$ and leave only $\delta_B$ and a small $\delta'$ to be multiplied by $e^{\varepsilon_L}$, which would lower $\kappa_L$ substantially. It needs a new proof of the hybrids and is not used here.
\end{remark}

\section{Input privacy, cohort privacy and correctness}
\label{sec:input}
% ==============================================================

\paragraph{G1.} $\Op$ sees $\Rp$'s seeded uploads $c_0 = -a s_R + e + \Ecd(\mathbf v)$, which are pseudorandom under RLWE in $s_R$ (with circular security covering $\Rp$'s rotation-key shares, and the PRG modelled as a random oracle); the PSI messages, which are BFV ciphertexts under $\Rp$'s own key and blinded OPRF elements; and $\Rp$'s key-generation shares, which are RLWE samples. $\Op$ never receives a decryption share from $\Rp$, so its view can be simulated from $(n_R^{\max}, \bar n_O)$, up to the failure probability of cuckoo hashing.

\paragraph{G2.} $\Op$ evaluates exactly $n_R^{\max}$ OPRF calls on blinded inputs and answers padded PSI queries, so it learns nothing about the cohort or $n_R$.

\paragraph{G4.} $\Rp$ learns $I$ and $\pi|_I$ from linkage. That it learns nothing else about $\Op$'s register needs the labeled PSI to be secure against a semi-honest receiver. Chen, Laine and Rindal prove their PSI from homomorphic encryption secure in the semi-honest model assuming the scheme is IND-CPA secure and circuit private, and note that statistical function privacy, obtained in the leveled setting by re-randomization and noise flooding, suffices~\cite{chen2017fast}. Chen, Huang, Laine and Rindal add OPRF preprocessing, which strengthens the security model~\cite{chen2018labeled}. Two layers of the protocol address what the noise of a reply could reveal.
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, label=(\alph*)]
  \item \emph{OPRF-keyed items and labels.} Every item $\Op$ inserts is $F_k(\mathrm{id}_j)$ under a fresh key, and every label is $\tau(j)$ masked by a key derived from $F'_k(\mathrm{id}_j)$. $\Rp$ learns $F_k$ only at its $n_R^{\max}$ queries, so the item and the label of every row outside $I$ are pseudorandom to it; $\tau$ is a uniform permutation, and the masks $\mathbf r_1$, $\mathbf r_2$ are fresh. A reply, noise included, is a deterministic function of $\Rp$'s ciphertexts and of these values. A simulator that knows $(I, \pi|_I)$, the public sizes and $\Rp$'s view of the OPRF can therefore run $\Op$'s evaluation on fresh random items and labels outside $I$ and obtain replies indistinguishable from the real ones, under the security of the OPRF. Even without circuit privacy, then, what the noise reveals about $\Op$'s register beyond $(I, \pi|_I)$ is pseudorandom.
  \item \textsc{SanitizeBFV} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:sub}) is part of the protocol nevertheless, so that G4 does not rest on the pseudorandomness of the evaluation's inputs alone. It switches each reply to the reply modulus $q'$, adds a fresh encryption of zero under $\Rp$'s BFV key, and floods the noise with an exact discrete Gaussian $\mathcal D_{\Z^{N_B}, \sigma_\psi}$. Let $B_\psi$ bound, with probability at least $1 - \delta_{B,\psi}$ and for every input of $\Op$, the $\ell_2$ norm of the switched reply's error other than the flooding noise. The requirements are
  \[
    \sigma_\psi \ge \max\{2^{\kappa_\psi}B_\psi/\sqrt{2\pi},\ 2^{20}\} \ \text{(flooding)}, \qquad
    q'/(2t) > t_\eta\,\sigma_\psi + B_\psi \ \text{(exact decryption)}.
  \]
  Then, as in hybrids H2 and H3 of Theorem~\ref{thm:output}, each reply ciphertext is within statistical distance $\delta_{B,\psi} + 2^{-\kappa_\psi}(1 + 2^{-80}) + 2^{-97}$, plus a computational term (RLWE in the randomness of the fresh encryption), of a ciphertext computed from its plaintext and fresh randomness alone. Over the $n_\psi$ reply ciphertexts of a run the distances add. The plaintexts themselves are $0$ or uniform, and a label or uniform, because of the masks.
\end{enumerate}
Neither layer is charged to $\delta$: the PSI transcript does not depend on $(\bx_O, y)$, so it lies outside the DP guarantee, and $\kappa_\psi$ is a security parameter of G4 alone. A modulus switch alone, as in the specification, is heuristic. Flooding protects against a semi-honest receiver, whose ciphertexts are fresh; a malicious receiver could send noisier ones, which is outside our model.

At the evaluation settings the implementation uses $\kappa_\psi = 40$, $\sigma_\psi = 2^{\kappa_\psi}B_\psi \approx 2^{61}$ with $B_\psi = 2(\sqrt{N_B}/2 + N_B^{3/2}/2) + 1 \approx 2^{21}$, and $q'$ of two 60-bit limbs, so $q'/(2t) \approx 2^{87}$ against $10\,\sigma_\psi \approx 2^{64.3}$. Both requirements hold for the rounding terms of the switch, which this $B_\psi$ bounds in the worst case for a ternary key. The switched evaluation noise is covered by the $+1$ only by measurement ($2^{-72}$ after the switch), not by a worst-case bound.

The circuit-privacy statements of CLR17 and the role of OPRF preprocessing in CHLR18 were checked only through search-engine extracts; confirm against the full texts, including whether CHLR18 state that OPRF preprocessing removes the need for circuit privacy.}

\paragraph{Correctness.} With $q_0$ from \textsc{Calibrate}, honest inputs never wrap around; the failure probability is at most $\delta_B + 2Ne^{-t_\eta^2/2}$ per release, a correctness event that is not charged to $\delta$. With the noise switched off, decryption reproduces the plaintext statistics up to the CKKS approximation error (Section~\ref{sec:eval:crypto}).

\subsection{Estimands}
\label{sec:stats:estimands}

The protocol targets two quantities.
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item The \textbf{ridge target} $\btheta_\lambda = (\bG + \lambda\Psi)^{-1}\bc$, a fixed function of the matched data once $(\lambda, \Psi)$ is chosen. Only the mechanism noise enters its uncertainty.
  \item The \textbf{regression coefficient} $\bbeta$ of the working linear model for the clipped data, whose sample analogue is $\hat\bbeta = \bG^{-1}\bc$. Its uncertainty includes sampling variance, and $\btheta_\lambda$ is biased for it when $\lambda > 0$.
\end{itemize}
Either the working model (conditionally independent, homoskedastic errors) is assumed for the clipped data---it holds if the bounds are true bounds, so that clipping never binds---or $\bbeta$ is the projection coefficient $\bbeta^\ast = \bG^{-1}\tilde\bX^\top\E[\tilde\by \mid \tilde\bX]$ of the clipped data. In the second case the bias bound below remains valid but the homoskedastic variance does not, because clipping induces heteroskedasticity.

\tocheck{Choose the definition of beta (working model on clipped data, or projection coefficient) and say what the bias-aware interval guarantees under heteroskedasticity, which is still open.}

\subsection{Stage 1: choosing the ridge}
\label{sec:stats:stage1}

Stage~1 releases $\hat\bB_1$ and $\hat\bC_1$ with s.d.\ $\sigma_1$, and $\Rp$ forms $\hat\bG_1$ with its exact $\bA$. With $r_1 = \sigma_1(2\sqrt p + 2\sqrt{\ln(1/\alpha_L)})$,
\[
  \Pr\bigl[\lambda_{\max}(\hat\bG_1 - \bG) > r_1\bigr] \le \alpha_L ,
\]
so $\hat L = \max\{0, \lambda_{\min}(\hat\bG_1) - r_1\} \le \lambda_{\min}(\bG)$ with probability at least $1 - \alpha_L$. This one-sided event is the only one the procedure uses; a two-sided statement needs $\ln(2/\alpha_L)$. At $p = 11$ and $\alpha_L = 0.0025$ (the evaluation setting) the radius is about $1.55$ times conservative.

\tocheck{r-1: optionally set it to the exact (1 - alpha-L) quantile of lambda-max of the noise, computed offline by Monte Carlo (7.43 sigma-1 instead of 11.53 sigma-1 for p = 11), at no privacy cost.}

With the target $g^\star = 2\vartheta^\star\sigma_2\sqrt p$, the full ridge
\[
  \lambda_I = \max\{0,\ g^\star + r_1 - \lambda_{\min}(\hat\bG_1)\}
\]
guarantees $\lambda_{\min}(\bG + \lambda_I\bI) \ge g^\star$ on that event. The $\Op$-block ridge $\lambda_O$ is the smallest $\lambda$ with $\lambda_{\min}(\hat\bG_1 + \lambda\PiO) \ge g^\star + r_1$. It is finite iff $\lambda_{\min}(\bA) > g^\star + r_1$, and the $\PiO$ ridge can never certify a validity ratio above $\lambda_{\min}(\bA)/(2\sigma_2\sqrt p)$. The rule takes $(\lambda, \Psi) = (\lambda_O, \PiO)$ if $\lambda_O \le \kappa_\Psi\lambda_I$, for a parameter $\kappa_\Psi \ge 0$, and $(\lambda_I, \bI)$ otherwise. Shrinking only $\Op$'s block leaves $\Rp$'s coefficients unshrunk when $\Rp$'s own block is well conditioned.

\begin{proposition}[Independence]
\label{prop:indep}
$(\lambda, \Psi)$ is a function of the stage-1 release and of $\bA$ only, and is therefore independent of the stage-2 noise.
\end{proposition}

This is what makes the bias theory of Section~\ref{sec:bias} and the intervals below valid conditionally on $(\lambda, \Psi)$. Choosing $\lambda$ from the same release that is used for estimation would make $\lambda$ a function of the noise; Section~\ref{sec:bias:lambda} shows what goes wrong.

\tocheck{The evaluation settings f-1 = 0.1, theta-star = 2.5 and kappa-Psi = 2 were chosen by simulation of earlier variants; confirm them under the two-stage rule.}

\subsection{Stage 2: estimate and gate}
\label{sec:stats:stage2}

Stage~2 releases every entry with s.d.\ $\sigma_2$. $\Rp$ assembles $\hat\bG_2$ and $\hat\bc_2$, computes the validity ratio $\hat\vartheta = \lambda_{\min}(\hat\bG_2 + \lambda\Psi)/(2\sigma_2\sqrt p)$, and releases nothing if $\hat\vartheta < 1$. Otherwise
\[
  \tilde\btheta = \tilde\bP\hat\bc_2, \qquad \tilde\bP = (\hat\bG_2 + \lambda\Psi)^{-1}.
\]
The gate is post-processing; a refused release still spends the budget. For $\vartheta^\star > 2$ the probability of refusal is at most $\alpha_L + \exp(-p(\vartheta^\star - 2)^2)$ (proofs, Part~B, Lemma~S3).

\subsection{Intervals}
\label{sec:stats:ci}

\paragraph{Mechanism variance.} $\Rp$ knows the noise distribution, so it estimates the covariance of $\tilde\btheta$ due to the mechanism by a parametric bootstrap around its own release: symmetric noise $\mathbf E^\ast$ with s.d.\ $\sigma_2$ on the $\Op$-dependent entries of $\hat\bG_2$ and zero on $\bA$, noise $\mathbf f^\ast \sim \mathcal N(0, \sigma_2^2\bI)$ on $\hat\bc_2$, and $\btheta^\ast_b = (\hat\bG_2 + \mathbf E^\ast_b + \lambda\Psi)^{-1}(\hat\bc_2 + \mathbf f^\ast_b)$. A draw is kept only if $\|\tilde\bP^{1/2}\mathbf E^\ast\tilde\bP^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \eta_B$, for a truncation parameter $\eta_B \in (0, 1)$; without truncation the bootstrap moments do not exist. With $B$ draws the Monte Carlo error of $\bV_{\mathrm{mech}}$ is of relative order $B^{-1/2}$. Antithetic pairs $(\pm\mathbf E^\ast, \pm\mathbf f^\ast)$ reduce Monte Carlo error. $\bV_{\mathrm{mech}}$ is the sample covariance of the kept draws. To first order, $\tilde\btheta - \btheta_\lambda \approx \tilde\bP(\bff - \bE\btheta_\lambda)$, so
\[
  \bV_{\mathrm{mech}} \approx \sigma_2^2\,\tilde\bP\bigl(\bI + K(\tilde\btheta)\bigr)\tilde\bP, \qquad
  K(\btheta)_{ii} = \textstyle\sum_j \chi_{ij}\theta_j^2, \quad K(\btheta)_{ik} = \chi_{ik}\theta_i\theta_k \ (i \ne k),
\]
with $\chi_{ij} = 0$ iff $i$ and $j$ both lie in $\Rp$'s block. $\Rp$ may use this closed form instead of the bootstrap.

\paragraph{Wald interval for $\btheta_\lambda$.} The error level is split as $\alpha = \alpha_{\mathrm{CI}} + \alpha_L + \alpha_U$, a parameter (the evaluation setting is $\alpha = 0.05$ with $\alpha_L = \alpha_U = \alpha/20$, so $\alpha_{\mathrm{CI}} = 0.045$ and $z_{1 - \alpha_{\mathrm{CI}}/2} = 2.005$). The interval is
\[
  \tilde\theta_j \pm z_{1-\alpha_{\mathrm{CI}}/2}\sqrt{(\bV_{\mathrm{mech}})_{jj}} .
\]

\paragraph{Residual scale.} $\widetilde{\mathrm{RSS}} = \widehat{yy} - \hat\bc_2^\top\tilde\btheta - \lambda\tilde\btheta^\top\Psi\tilde\btheta$ is biased by the noise at order $\sigma_2^2$. The same bootstrap gives $\mathrm{Corr} = \overline{\mathrm{RSS}^\ast} - \widetilde{\mathrm{RSS}}$ and $\tilde s^2 = \max\{\widetilde{\mathrm{RSS}} - \mathrm{Corr}, 0\}/(n - p)$, which removes the $O(\sigma_2^2)$ bias up to $O(\sigma_2^4)$.

\paragraph{Bias-aware interval for $\bbeta$.} The total variance adds the sampling term, $\bV = \bV_{\mathrm{mech}} + \tilde s^2\,\tilde\bP\hat\bG_2\tilde\bP$. The ridge bias of coordinate $j$ is at most $\bar b_j = \lambda\|\Psi\tilde\bP\mathbf e_j\|_2\,\hat B_\beta$, where $\hat B_\beta = \min\{B_\beta^{\mathrm{pub}}, \sqrt{U_{yy}/\hat L}\}$ bounds $\|\bbeta\|$ from a public a-priori bound and a private one,
\[
  U_{yy} = \min\Bigl\{\widehat{yy} + z_{1-\alpha_U/2}\,\sigma_{yy} + B_y^2\sqrt{n\ln(2/\alpha_U)/2},\ nB_y^2\Bigr\},
\]
with $\sigma_{yy}$ the s.d.\ of the release that carries $\by^\top\by$. The interval, following Armstrong, Kolesár and Kwon~\cite{armstrong2020bias}, is
\[
  \tilde\theta_j \pm \mathrm{cv}_{\alpha_{\mathrm{CI}}}\bigl(\bar b_j/\sqrt{V_{jj}}\bigr)\sqrt{V_{jj}},
\]
where $\mathrm{cv}_\alpha(t)$ is the $(1 - \alpha)$ quantile of $|Z + t|$ for $Z \sim \mathcal N(0, 1)$. The private bound is usually infinite while the ridge is on ($\hat L = 0$), so without a public $B_\beta^{\mathrm{pub}}$ the interval for $\bbeta$ is unbounded exactly when the ridge is needed. This is the honest answer: the data cannot rule out large coefficients in the directions the ridge suppresses.

\begin{remark}[How accurate is first order?]
\label{rem:firstorder}
The validity ratio controls convergence of the Neumann series, but the relative size of the neglected mechanism bias in the Wald interval is of order $\sigma_2\tr(\bP_\lambda) \le \sqrt p/(2\vartheta_0)$, which at $\vartheta^\star = 2.5$ and $p = 11$ (the evaluation setting) can be $0.66$. Coverage claims are therefore asymptotic as $\vartheta_0 \to \infty$ at fixed $p$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:solve} reports $\sigma_2\tr(\tilde\bP)$ as a diagnostic.
\end{remark}

\subsection{When can the ridge be dropped?}
\label{sec:stats:noff}

The stage-1 rule is explicit, so the intersection size from which it stops adding a ridge can be read off the protocol parameters and one property of the data.

\paragraph{The threshold.} Let $\Sigma = \E[\bx\bx^\top]$ be the per-record second-moment matrix of the preprocessed, clipped design, intercept included, and $\ell = \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)$ its weakest direction. With $n$ matched records $\bG \approx n\Sigma$ and $\lambda_{\min}(\bG) \approx n\ell$, up to a relative fluctuation of order $n^{-1/2}$. Ignoring the stage-1 noise in $\lambda_{\min}(\hat\bG_1)$, the full ridge is $\lambda_I(n) \approx \max\{0,\ g^\star + r_1 - n\ell\}$, which falls linearly in $n$ and vanishes at
\begin{equation}
  n_{\mathrm{off}} = \frac{g^\star + r_1}{\ell}
  = \frac{\sqrt p}{\ell\sqrt{2\rho_{\mathrm{run}}}}\left[\frac{2\vartheta^\star\Delta}{\sqrt{1 - f_1}} + \Bigl(2 + 2\sqrt{\ln(1/\alpha_L)/p}\Bigr)\frac{\Delta_G}{\sqrt{f_1}}\right],
  \label{eq:noff}
\end{equation}
by~\eqref{eq:sigmas}. At the evaluation settings ($\vartheta^\star = 2.5$, $f_1 = 0.1$, $\alpha_L = 0.0025$) and $p = 11$ this is
\[
  n_{\mathrm{off}} \approx \frac{17.5\,\Delta + 36.5\,\Delta_G}{\ell\sqrt{2\rho_{\mathrm{run}}}},
\]
with $\sqrt{2\rho_{\mathrm{run}}} = 0.089$, $0.173$, $0.335$, $0.642$ and $1.21$ for $\varepsilon = 0.5$, $1$, $2$, $4$ and $8$ at $\delta = 10^{-9}$. Since $\sqrt{\rho_{\mathrm{run}}}$ grows almost linearly in $\varepsilon$ at fixed $\delta$, $n_{\mathrm{off}}$ scales like the sensitivity divided by $\ell\varepsilon$. The two terms have different origins. $g^\star/\ell$ is the threshold if $\ell$ were known in advance; $r_1/\ell$ is the price of learning it from stage~1. With $\Delta_G \approx 0.9\Delta$, as for the simulation population, $r_1 \approx 1.9g^\star$, so learning $\ell$ multiplies the threshold by about $2.9$. The private bound $\hat L$ on $\lambda_{\min}(\bG)$, which makes the interval for $\bbeta$ finite without a public bound on $\|\bbeta\|$, becomes positive earlier, from $n \approx r_1/\ell$ (about $0.65\,n_{\mathrm{off}}$ there).

\paragraph{Below the threshold.} With $\Psi = \bI$, the component of $\btheta_\lambda$ along an eigenvector of $\bG$ with eigenvalue $n\ell_k$ keeps the fraction $n\ell_k/(n\ell_k + \lambda)$ of the least-squares coefficient. In the weakest direction $n\ell + \lambda \approx g^\star + r_1 = n_{\mathrm{off}}\ell$, so the fraction kept is $n/n_{\mathrm{off}}$: the shrinkage there falls linearly in $n$ and is gone at $n_{\mathrm{off}}$. Directions with $\ell_k \gg \ell$ are barely shrunk. A weak direction carries little of the variation in $\bx$, so shrinking it costs little in prediction error but a lot in the coefficients that load on it; this is why prediction recovers before the coefficients do (Section~\ref{sec:eval:e2e}).

\paragraph{The transition.} The noise lowers $\lambda_{\min}(\hat\bG_1)$ on average, the more so when several eigenvalues of $\Sigma$ are close to $\ell$, so the rule chooses a slightly larger $\lambda$ than the formula and switches off slightly after $n_{\mathrm{off}}$. Table~\ref{tab:noff_sim} simulates the stage-1 rule on the simulation population of Section~\ref{sec:eval:sim} ($\ell = 0.598$, $\Delta = 86.2$, $\Delta_G = 77.2$, $p = 11$): the ridge is never off at $0.8\,n_{\mathrm{off}}$, off in $1$--$3\%$ of releases at $n_{\mathrm{off}}$ and in $96$--$98\%$ at $1.2\,n_{\mathrm{off}}$; a repeat on fresh cohorts gives $3$--$5\%$ and $94$--$99\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:noff_check_v31}). We take $1.2\,n_{\mathrm{off}}$ as the size from which the ridge is off with high probability. Table~\ref{tab:calib_example} uses the committed bound $\ell = 0.494$ instead of the population value, so its thresholds are $21\%$ higher. Under the earlier mechanism, which used that committed bound instead of a stage-1 release, the ridge switched off at exactly $g^\star/\ell$ for that mechanism's $\sigma$ and $\vartheta^\star = 2$.

% ==============================================================
\section{Mechanism bias and the optional correction}
\label{sec:bias}
% ==============================================================

Throughout this section $\sigma$ denotes the stage-2 slot s.d.\ $\sigma_2$, and $(\lambda, \Psi)$ is fixed independently of the stage-2 noise (Proposition~\ref{prop:indep}).

\subsection{Noise model}

Write the stage-2 statistics as $\hat\bG_2 = \bG + \bE$ and $\hat\bc_2 = \bc + \bff$, with
\begin{equation}
  \bE = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{0} & \bE_{C} \\ \bE_{C}^{\top} & \bE_{B} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
  \bff = \begin{bmatrix} \bff_{R} \\ \bff_{O} \end{bmatrix}.
  \label{eq:noise_structure}
\end{equation}
The top-left block of $\bE$ is identically zero, since $\bA$ is exact. Each independent entry on and above the diagonal of the $\Op$-dependent blocks is drawn with variance $\sigma^{2}$ and mirrored, so every entry, the diagonal included, has variance $\sigma^{2}$. With $\chi_{ij} = 0$ iff $i, j$ both lie in $\Rp$'s block and $\chi_{ij} = 1$ otherwise,
\begin{equation}
  \E[E_{ij}E_{kl}] = \sigma^{2}\chi_{ij}\bigl(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl} + \delta_{il}\delta_{jk} - \delta_{ij}\delta_{ik}\delta_{il}\bigr).
  \label{eq:noise_cov}
\end{equation}
The last term removes the diagonal double-count of the GOE convention. $\bE$ and $\bff$ occupy disjoint entries of the release, are independent, and are symmetric about zero. (The lattice noise induces slot-domain noise with these first and second moments; the CKKS error is flooded and of lower order.)

Condition on the realized sample, so $\hat{\bbeta} = \bG^{-1}\bc$ is fixed, and set $\bP = \bG^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}
  \vartheta_0 = \frac{\lambda_{\min}(\bG)}{2\sigma\sqrt{p}}, \qquad
  \mathcal{E}_{\eta} = \bigl\{\|\bG^{-1/2}\bE\bG^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \eta\bigr\} \quad (0 < \eta < 1).
  \label{eq:event}
\end{equation}
$\mathcal{E}_{\eta}$ is invariant under $\bE \mapsto -\bE$. Define
\begin{equation}
  M(\bP) = \bigl(\bP^{2} + \tr(\bP)\bP - \bP\diag(\bP)\bigr)\PiO + \bigl(\bP\PiO\bP + \tr(\PiO\bP)\bP\bigr)\PiR,
  \label{eq:M}
\end{equation}
where $\diag(\bP)$ is the diagonal matrix formed from the diagonal of $\bP$.

\begin{remark}[The unconditional mean does not exist]
With Gaussian $\bE$, $(\bG + \bE)^{-1}(\bc + \bff)$ has no finite mean: already for $p = 1$, $\E[1/(a + e)]$ diverges. Statements about its bias must therefore be conditional, or about a truncated estimator. We condition on $\mathcal{E}_{\eta}$, whose complement has exponentially small probability.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Result}

\begin{theorem}[Leading-order conditional bias]
\label{thm:bias}
Fix $\eta \in (0,1)$ with $\eta\vartheta_0 > 1$. Then, for $\tilde\bbeta = (\bG + \bE)^{-1}(\bc + \bff)$,
\[
  \E\bigl[\tilde{\bbeta} \mid \mathcal{E}_{\eta}\bigr] - \hat{\bbeta}
  = \sigma^{2} M(\bP)\,\hat{\bbeta} + O(\sigma^{4}) + O\bigl(e^{-p(\eta\vartheta_0 - 1)^{2}/2}\bigr),
\]
where the constants depend on $\|\bP\|_{\mathrm{op}}$, $\|\hat{\bbeta}\|$, $p$ and $\eta$. The moment-vector noise $\bff$ contributes nothing at this order; it is variance-only. The term $-\bP\diag(\bP)\PiO$ is the single-draw correction to the GOE contraction.
\end{theorem}

The proof is given in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}. Setting $\PiO \to \bI$ and $\PiR \to \mathbf{0}$ recovers the homogeneous-Gram bias, the correct check when every block is noised.

\begin{remark}[When the expansion is valid]
\label{rem:validity}
$\bP\bE$ is similar to $\bG^{-1/2}\bE\bG^{-1/2}$, so the Neumann series for $(\bI + \bP\bE)^{-1}$ converges iff $-\bG \prec \bE \prec \bG$; on $\mathcal{E}_{\eta}$ it converges geometrically with ratio $\eta$. $\|\bE\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ concentrates at or below $2\sigma\sqrt{p}$ with sub-Gaussian tails, so $\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{\eta}^{c}) \le \exp(-p(\eta\vartheta_0 - 1)^{2})$ for $\eta\vartheta_0 > 1$. Since $\lambda_{\min}(\bG)$ grows linearly in $n$ while $\sigma$ does not depend on $n$, the condition holds with high probability once $n$ is large enough; below that, a ridge is required.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Schur-complement reading}

Let $\bS = \bB - \bC^{\top}\bA^{-1}\bC$ be the Schur complement of $\bA$ in $\bG$. Block inversion gives $\PiO\bP\PiO = \bS^{-1}$, hence
\begin{equation}
  \tr(\PiO\bP) = \tr(\bS^{-1}), \qquad
  \tr(\bP) = \tr(\bA^{-1}) + \tr\bigl(\bA^{-1}\bC\bS^{-1}\bC^{\top}\bA^{-1}\bigr) + \tr(\bS^{-1}).
  \label{eq:traces}
\end{equation}
The two trace weights governing the leading bias are $\tr(\bG^{-1})$ for the $\Op$-coefficients and $\tr(\bS^{-1})$ for the $\Rp$-coefficients. When $\Op$'s features carry little signal beyond $\Rp$'s, $\bS$ is near-singular and the private estimator is worst-biased in $\Rp$'s own coefficients.

\subsection{Ridge}

Let $\bG_{\lambda} = \bG + \lambda\Psi$, $\bP_{\lambda} = \bG_{\lambda}^{-1}$, and let $\vartheta_{\lambda}$ and $\mathcal{E}_{\eta,\lambda}$ be as in~\eqref{eq:event} with $\bG$ replaced by $\bG_{\lambda}$.

\begin{theorem}[Ridge mechanism bias]
\label{thm:ridge}
Suppose $(\lambda, \Psi)$ is independent of $(\bE, \bff)$ and $\eta\vartheta_{\lambda} > 1$. Then
\[
  \E\bigl[\tilde{\btheta} \mid \mathcal{E}_{\eta,\lambda}\bigr] - \btheta_{\lambda}
  = \sigma^{2} M(\bP_{\lambda})\,\btheta_{\lambda} + O(\sigma^{4}) + O\bigl(e^{-p(\eta\vartheta_{\lambda} - 1)^{2}/2}\bigr),
\]
with the moment-vector noise $\bff$ again variance-only.
\end{theorem}

The proof is that of Theorem~\ref{thm:bias} under $\bG \mapsto \bG_{\lambda}$, which leaves $\bE$, its covariance~\eqref{eq:noise_cov} and $\bff$ untouched.

\begin{proposition}[Total bias against least squares: shrinkage split]
\label{prop:shrink}
If the estimand is $\hat{\bbeta} = \bG^{-1}\bc$, the total bias of the private ridge splits as
\[
  \E[\tilde{\btheta} \mid \mathcal{E}_{\eta,\lambda}] - \hat{\bbeta}
  = \underbrace{\btheta_{\lambda} - \hat{\bbeta}}_{\text{deterministic}}
  + \underbrace{\E[\tilde{\btheta} \mid \mathcal{E}_{\eta,\lambda}] - \btheta_{\lambda}}_{\text{mechanism}},
  \qquad
  \btheta_{\lambda} - \hat{\bbeta} = -\lambda\bP_{\lambda}\Psi\hat{\bbeta}.
\]
The deterministic term grows in $\lambda$; the mechanism term decays like $\sigma^{2}/\lambda$.
\end{proposition}

For $\Psi = \bI$, $\lambda_{\min}(\bG_{\lambda}) = \lambda_{\min}(\bG) + \lambda$ grows without bound. For $\Psi = \PiO$, $\lambda_{\min}(\bG + \lambda\PiO) \to \lambda_{\min}(\bA)$ as $\lambda \to \infty$, so the $\Op$-block ridge cannot validate the expansion when $\lambda_{\min}(\bA)$ is too small, which is why the stage-1 rule falls back to $\Psi = \bI$.

\subsection{Why the ridge must not depend on the estimation noise}
\label{sec:bias:lambda}

Earlier drafts chose $(\lambda, \Psi)$ from the same noisy Gram matrix used for estimation. That choice is post-processing and costs no privacy, but it makes $\lambda$ a function of $\bE$ at first order, so Theorem~\ref{thm:ridge} no longer applies. In simulation, with $\lambda$ fixed independently the correction below removed $87$--$98\%$ of the mechanism bias, whereas under the adaptive rule it removed essentially none: the residual after correction was $1.0$--$1.6$ times the uncorrected bias. The two-stage rule of Section~\ref{sec:stats:stage1} keeps the adaptivity and restores independence.

\subsection{Optional zero-budget correction}
\label{sec:bias:correction}

Since $\sigma$, $d_{R}$, $d_{O}$ and $\PiO$ are public and $\hat\bG_2$ is already private, the bias is estimable by post-processing:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\btheta}_{\mathrm{bc}} = \tilde{\btheta} - \sigma^{2}M(\tilde{\bP})\,\tilde{\btheta}.
  \label{eq:bc}
\end{equation}
Substituting $\hat\bG_2$ for $\bG$ inside a term already of order $\sigma^{2}$ injects only $O(\sigma^{3})$ fluctuation and $O(\sigma^{4})$ bias. One must not additionally invert the deterministic shrinkage, which would re-amplify precisely the directions the ridge protects. In practice the bias removed is small: in our evaluation the mechanism bias of one run is at most $0.05\%$ of the mean squared error (Section~\ref{sec:eval:bias}). The correction is therefore an option of the protocol, off in our evaluation.

\section{Prediction after training}
\label{sec:inference}
% ==============================================================

\tocheck{This section is carried over from the earlier draft and adapted to threshold keys. Split inference is not part of the v3.1 specification: the sensitivity, noise and ledger charge of a prediction release are still to be defined.}

After a run, $\Rp$ holds $\tilde\btheta = (\tilde\btheta_{R}; \tilde\btheta_{O})$. For a new individual $i$, $\hat{y}_{i} = \bxd_{i}^{\top}\tilde\btheta_{R} + \bx_{O,i}^{\top}\tilde\btheta_{O}$; $\Rp$ can evaluate the first term but has no access to $\bx_{O,i}$.

\paragraph{Approach 1: split inference.} $\Rp$ encrypts $\tilde\btheta_{O}$ under the joint key; after linkage on the test identifiers, $\Op$ computes $\Enc{p_{O,i}} = \sum_k X_{O,ik}\Enc{\tilde\theta_{O,k}}$ and releases it with Algorithm~\ref{alg:release}. Threshold decryption fits this use well. Every prediction needs $\Op$'s share, so $\Op$ adds noise to each batch and charges it to the ledger. Re-randomization prevents the attack in which $\Rp$ solves the linear system in $c_1$ for $\bx_{O,i}$ exactly. Two leaks remain and must be priced: each decrypted $p_{O,i}$ is a known-direction projection of $\bx_{O,i}$; and across $k$ repeated queries or $T \ge d_O$ model versions, averaging or solving the projections reconstructs $\bx_{O,i}$ unless every query and retraining is charged.

\paragraph{Approach 2: distillation to an $\Rp$-only model.} The student coefficient that regresses the joint model's fitted values on $\Rp$'s features is $\hat{\bbeta}_{R}^{\mathrm{st}} = \bA^{-1}\bcR = \bbeta_{R} + \bA^{-1}\bC\bbeta_{O}$. $\Rp$ computes $\bA^{-1}\hat{\bc}_{R}$ from its exact $\bA$ and the released $\hat\bc_R$, which is post-processing and needs no interaction at test time. Its loss against the joint fitted values is $\bbeta_{O}^{\top}\bS\bbeta_{O}$, the quadratic form of $\bbeta_O$ in the Schur complement: when it is small, distillation suffices; when large, $\Op$'s features carry signal that $\Rp$'s cannot proxy.

\section{Parameters}
\label{sec:params}
% ==============================================================

The protocol is defined for a parameter vector $\mathcal P$. Table~\ref{tab:params} lists every parameter: its role, the constraint it must satisfy, and the value used in this paper's evaluation. The last column is an \emph{evaluation setting}, one admissible choice among many; it is not part of the protocol.

The constraints are of three kinds. Some are hypotheses that $\mathcal P$ itself must meet, such as the ranges of the shares and the fact that the bounds and the preprocessing constants are public and fixed in advance; the parties check them in Setup. Some involve quantities that \textsc{Calibrate} derives: the existence of $\kappa$, $\delta_{\mathrm{DP}} > 0$, a scale that floods, the size of $q_0$ and the security bound on the chain. \textsc{Calibrate} checks these and refuses the run otherwise (Section~\ref{sec:privacy:calib}). The rest, such as $f_1$, $\vartheta^\star$ and $\kappa_\Psi$, affect only utility or cost and need only lie in their ranges. We call $\mathcal P$ \emph{admissible} if all constraints of Table~\ref{tab:params} hold. Theorem~\ref{thm:output}, Proposition~\ref{prop:compose}, the input-privacy and correctness statements of Section~\ref{sec:input} and the statistical results of Section~\ref{sec:stats} hold for every admissible $\mathcal P$, with the constants they state; none of them uses a particular value.

\section{Empirical evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

All results in this section are for protocol v3.1: its calibration, the full OpenFHE implementation (Sections~\ref{sec:eval:crypto} and~\ref{sec:eval:e2e}), and plaintext simulations of its release and solve steps, which reproduce the encrypted runs (Sections~\ref{sec:eval:e2e} and~\ref{sec:eval:sim}). Where a subsection compares with the earlier single-release mechanism of the previous version of this paper (one analytic-Gaussian release, a ridge set from a committed eigenvalue bound, and the bias correction applied), it says so. The scripts that produce every number are in the \texttt{experiments/} directory of the project repository.

\subsection{The cryptographic layer is exact}
\label{sec:eval:crypto}

A prototype of the linkage and aggregation steps was run end to end over sockets, on six cohorts ($n = 200$ to $64{,}000$ matched records out of $n_O = 2{,}000$ to $80{,}000$) with a plaintext backend, and on one cohort ($n = 400$ out of $n_O = 4{,}000$) with OpenFHE~1.5.1 (BFV, $N = 16{,}384$, for the linkage; CKKS with scale $2^{50}$ for the aggregation). Labeled PSI recovered exactly the true match set in every run, with no misaligned rows (count, set and row-by-row alignment checked). With the noise switched off, the regression agreed with its plaintext target to $2.9 \times 10^{-10}$ with the plaintext backend and $1.7 \times 10^{-10}$ with OpenFHE. The OpenFHE run took about 13~s end to end. The prototype hashes identifiers with SHA-256 instead of an OPRF, uses a single CKKS key, and has no release step.

\paragraph{Full implementation of v3.1.} The \texttt{implementation/} directory of the repository implements every phase of v3.1 on OpenFHE~1.5.1, with $\Rp$ and $\Op$ as separate processes and a transcript of every message: RFC~9497 OPRF (ristretto255), labeled PSI under $\Rp$'s BFV key with flooded replies, threshold key generation, the seeded upload and key conversion, the aggregation circuit, and the release with exact discrete-Gaussian noise inside $\Op$'s share. A small C++ layer provides the polynomial-level access that the release needs. On the toy example and on a $4{,}096$-row synthetic register it recovers exactly the true match set and alignment; with the noise switched off by a test-only flag the decrypted statistics match their plaintext values to $10^{-11}$ relative; with the noise on, the measured noise s.d.\ is $0.996\,\sigma_2$ and $0.976\,\sigma_1$; the randomness-dependent encryption error stays about 8 bits below the worst-case $B_{\mathrm{tot}}$; and $\Rp$ never receives $c_0'$ while $\Op$ never receives anything that could serve as a decryption share of $\Rp$. Its deviations from the specification are listed in \texttt{implementation/DEVIATIONS.md}.

\subsection{End-to-end runs of the implementation}
\label{sec:eval:e2e}

These runs use the full implementation: OPRF and labeled PSI, threshold CKKS upload and aggregation, two releases with exact discrete-Gaussian noise inside $\Op$'s share, and the solver, with $\delta = 10^{-9}$ and about $40$--$45$\,s per run on one core per party. \subsection{Coefficient accuracy against intersection size}
\label{sec:eval:sim}

\paragraph{Setup.} The population has $d_{R} = 4$ and $d_{O} = 6$ features on realistic raw scales (a score, a binary indicator, lognormal income and assets, a count and an age), Toeplitz-correlated with coefficient $0.3$. They are standardized with public population constants and clipped to the population's $99.5\%$ bounds ($B_{R} = 5.59$ with the intercept, $B_{O} = 6.24$, $B_{y} = 2.89$ at $R^2 = 0.5$). The generating slopes give $R^{2} \in \{0.3, 0.5, 0.9\}$. The weakest direction of the clipped design is $\ell = \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma) = 0.599$, estimated from $4$ million population rows, and $\lambda_{\min}(\bA)/n = 0.656$.

The mechanism is v3.1, simulated in plaintext with the implementation's own calibration and solver: a stage-1 release of $(\triu\bB, \bC)$ with s.d.\ $\sigma_1$, the stage-1 rule of Section~\ref{sec:stats:stage1}, a stage-2 release of every $\Op$-dependent entry with s.d.\ $\sigma_2$, the gate $\hat\vartheta \ge 1$, and the solve, with the bias correction off. The simulated noise is Gaussian in the slot domain with the calibrated s.d. The encrypted implementation's measured noise is $0.996\,\sigma_2$ and $0.976\,\sigma_1$ (Section~\ref{sec:eval:crypto}), and its estimates on protein lie inside the simulated range (Section~\ref{sec:eval:e2e}). A further encrypted check in the ridge-on regime is reported at the end of this subsection.

The evaluation uses these settings, which are not protocol defaults: $\delta = 10^{-9}$, $f_1 = 0.1$, $\vartheta^\star = 2.5$, $\kappa_\Psi = 2$, $\alpha = 0.05$ with $\alpha_L = \alpha_U = \alpha/20$, $\by^\top\by$ in stage~2 (it is free for these bounds), and the flooding parameter $\kappa = 40$ for $\varepsilon \le 2$, $44$ at $\varepsilon = 4$ and $48$ at $\varepsilon = 8$. With $\Delta = 86.2$ and $\Delta_G = 77.2$ this gives $\sigma_1 = 2746$, $1411$, $729$, $380$, $202$ and $\sigma_2 = 1022$, $525$, $271$, $141$, $75$ for $\varepsilon = 0.5$, $1$, $2$, $4$, $8$ (Table~\ref{tab:noff_sim}). For each $n$ from $10^{2}$ to $10^{5}$ we draw 200 cohorts. For each cohort and each $\varepsilon$ we run an independent pair of releases, and we report the RMSE of the slopes against the true coefficients.

\subsection{Does the bias correction matter?}
\label{sec:eval:bias}

Under v3.1 the ridge comes from stage~1, so it is independent of the stage-2 noise (Proposition~\ref{prop:indep}), and Theorem~\ref{thm:ridge} applies conditionally on $(\lambda, \Psi)$. We ask the question again with the earlier conditional design, at the settings of Section~\ref{sec:eval:sim} ($\delta = 10^{-9}$, $R^2 = 0.5$).
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item For each $n \in \{500, 1{,}000, \dots, 128{,}000\}$ (doubling) and each $\varepsilon$ we draw five datasets.
  \item For each dataset we draw four stage-1 releases, each of which fixes $(\lambda, \Psi)$.
  \item For each of these we draw $10{,}000$ antithetic pairs $(\bE, \bff)$, $(-\bE, -\bff)$ of stage-2 releases.
\end{itemize}

The reason is the stage-1 rule: while the ridge is on it holds $\vartheta_\lambda$ at $7.9$--$9.2$, well above $\vartheta^\star = 2.5$, because of the radius $r_1$; in these cells the ridge is off only from $\vartheta_0 = 9.6$.   \item \emph{The theorem holds.} Wherever $\vartheta_0 \ge 1$, the simulated bias is $0.997$--$1.010$ times the leading term $\sigma_2^2 M(\bP_\lambda)\btheta_\lambda$.

  \item \emph{Real data.} On the 29 datasets of Section~\ref{sec:eval:adassp} the correction changed the test MSE of v3.1 by $-0.01\%$ to $+0.39\%$, against $-3.7\%$ to $+2.3\%$ before.

\subsection{Benchmark against AdaSSP}
\label{sec:eval:adassp}

AdaSSP~\cite{wang2018revisiting} perturbs the same sufficient statistics and solves a ridge whose penalty adapts to a private estimate of $\lambda_{\min}(\bX^{\top}\bX)$. It assumes one curator holding the joined table, which our setting rules out. The comparison therefore measures the utility cost of never pooling the data.

\paragraph{Setup.} We use the 29 UCI datasets~\cite{dua2019uci} of Wang's published evaluation, with 10-fold cross-validation on the folds of the earlier comparison. This subsection uses $\delta = 10^{-6}$, as Wang's published evaluation does. There are 50 noise draws per fold and $\varepsilon \in \{0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8\}$.
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \emph{Rows.} In each fold, $10\%$ of all rows, taken from the training folds, form a public reference set. Only the per-party preprocessing uses it, to fix its constants and bounds. Every arm trains on the remaining training rows ($80\%$ of the data) and is tested on the held-out fold.
  \item \emph{Split.} $\Rp$ holds the first $\lfloor d/2 \rfloor$ features and $\Op$ the rest and $y$. Every training row counts as matched.
\end{itemize}
We compare five arms.
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item[(a)] \emph{AdaSSP (published):} Wang's algorithm and noise constants, with his preprocessing: z-scored features, each whole row scaled to unit norm, $y/\max|y|$. Its guarantee covers the whole record $(\bx, y)$.
  \item[(b)] \emph{AdaSSP (matched):} AdaSSP's adaptive ridge under our adjacency and our accountant. $\bA$ is exact; $\rho_{\mathrm{run}}/3$ goes to $\lambda_{\min}$ and $2\rho_{\mathrm{run}}/3$ to one joint release of the $\Op$-dependent entries with $\Delta = \sqrt{10}$, with the tight conversion. It is idealized, because its private $\lambda_{\min}$ step needs $\bX^{\top}\bX$ in the clear.
  \item[(c)] \emph{v3.1 with joint row normalization:} v3.1 on Wang's preprocessed data, without an intercept, with $B_R = B_O = B_y = 1$. It is idealized too. Wang's normalization scales each row $(\bx_R, \bx_O)$ by its joint norm, which needs both parties' features in one row, and no party has them in the vertical setting. The arm isolates the change of mechanism from the change of preprocessing.
  \item[(d)] \emph{v3.1 with block unit norm:} each party normalizes its own block of every row (Section~\ref{sec:protocol:preproc}), so $B_R^2 = 2$ with $\Rp$'s intercept and $B_O = 1$. $B_y$ is the $99.5\%$ quantile on the reference set.
  \item[(e)] \emph{v3.1 with standardization:} each party standardizes its own columns with reference-set constants; the bounds are the $99.5\%$ quantiles on the reference set.
\end{itemize}
The v3.1 arms run both releases, the stage-1 rule, the gate and the solve, with the bias correction off; a refused release would output $\btheta = 0$, but none was refused. Arms with different preprocessing have different non-private baselines. Each arm is therefore reported in two ways:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item as a ratio of its test MSE to that of its own non-private fit, $(\bZ^\top\bZ + \bI)^{-1}\bZ^\top\by$ (Wang's non-private estimator), on the same matched rows with the same preprocessing; arms (a)--(c) share this fit;
  \item as an absolute test MSE on one common scale, Wang's $y/\max|y|$, which adds the cost of the preprocessing.
\end{itemize}
Methods are compared by paired tests over folds (95\% CI of the mean difference of absolute test MSE, $t$ with 9 degrees of freedom).

\begin{itemize}

Its median ridge at $\varepsilon = 1$ is $3.2$--$5.8$ times that of AdaSSP (matched) and $4.7$--$12$ times that of AdaSSP (published), for example $786$, $188$ and $127$ on housing and $559$, $150$ and $101$ on protein.   \item \emph{The preprocessing.} Block normalization (d) and joint normalization (c) differ significantly on only 12 of 29 datasets in absolute test MSE ($\varepsilon = 1$), and their ratios to their own non-private fits are similar. Their differences come from the non-private fits: on elevators, block normalization's non-private test MSE is $2.6$ times that of Wang's preprocessing, while on concrete it is $0.86$ times. Plain standardization (e) is worse than block normalization on 23--25 datasets, because heavy tails inflate the bounds.
  \item \emph{The implementable protocol.} Arm (d) has higher test MSE than AdaSSP (published) on 19 of 29 datasets at $\varepsilon = 1$ and lower on 5. The median gap is $+31\%$ at $\varepsilon = 1$ and $+12\%$ at $\varepsilon = 8$. On protein the gap is $3.6\%$ ($0.124$ against $0.120$), and on bike it is $34\%$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{AdaSSP's published noise constants.} AdaSSP adds noise of s.d.\ $s = \sqrt{\log(6/\delta)}/(\varepsilon/3)$ to each of three statistics: $\lambda_{\min}$, $\bX^\top\bX$ (symmetrized, so off-diagonal s.d.\ $s/\sqrt2$) and $\bX^\top\by$.
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item Under add/remove adjacency each of the three has sensitivity $1$ for unit-norm rows. The three releases then cost $\rho = 3/(2s^2)$, which the tight conversion turns into $(0.31, 0.64, 1.35, 2.87, 6.24)$-DP at $\delta = 10^{-6}$ for nominal $\varepsilon = (0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8)$, which is valid, with room.
  \item Under replace-one adjacency, which is ours, the sensitivities of $\bX^\top\bX$ (the Frobenius norm of $\bx\bx^\top - \bx'\bx'^\top$, which matches the symmetrized noise) and of $\bX^\top\by$ are $\sqrt2$ and $2$. The same constants then give $(0.48, 1.01, 2.14, 4.60, 10.2)$-DP.
  \item Recalibrated to exactly $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ under replace-one adjacency, with the tight conversion and an equal split, AdaSSP's test MSE rises by a median $2$--$3\%$ (range $-5\%$ to $+28\%$).
\end{itemize}
The published column therefore overstates AdaSSP's advantage only slightly. The gap to v3.1 lies in the ridge rule.

\subsection{Where the utility goes}
\label{sec:eval:lessons}

\paragraph{Prediction is not interpretation.} Test MSE includes the irreducible noise, so it is forgiving. On the simulation population, prediction comes within $10\%$ of non-private at about $0.71\,n_{\mathrm{off}}$, while the ridge is still on; coefficients within twice the non-private error take $1.7$--$5.2$ times as many records (Section~\ref{sec:eval:sim}). Intervals for $\bbeta$ are informative only once the ridge is off or a public bound on $\|\bbeta\|$ is available.

\paragraph{The gap to AdaSSP is the ridge, not the noise.} At the same $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ and unit bounds, the stage-2 noise of v3.1 is lower than that of AdaSSP under our guarantee ($\sigma_2 = 15.1$ against $17.6$ at $\varepsilon = 1$, $\delta = 10^{-6}$). Its ridge is $3$--$6$ times larger (Section~\ref{sec:eval:adassp}), because the stage-1 rule certifies the validity ratio $\vartheta^\star$ on which the intervals rest, from a release with a tenth of the budget and at confidence $1 - \alpha_L$. While it is on, the rule holds $\vartheta_\lambda$ near $8$--$9$ rather than at $\vartheta^\star = 2.5$: on the simulation population, where $\sigma_1 \approx 2.7\,\sigma_2$, the radius $r_1$ alone adds $r_1/(2\sigma_2\sqrt p) \approx 4.7$ to the target (Section~\ref{sec:eval:bias}). AdaSSP sizes its ridge for accuracy and gives no intervals. The levers of Section~\ref{sec:stats:noff} shrink the v3.1 ridge by about $37\%$; a setting meant only for prediction could also lower $\vartheta^\star$, giving up the interval guarantee. Neither has been evaluated yet. Per-party block normalization costs little against Wang's joint normalization, which no party can apply in the vertical setting: the two differ significantly on 12 of 29 datasets, mostly through their non-private fits.

\tocheck{Evaluate the interval-free prediction setting (smaller theta-star, exact-quantile r-1, larger f-1) against AdaSSP, and decide whether the paper presents two settings of the same protocol: one for inference and one for prediction.}

\paragraph{How well the data fill their bounds matters as much as $n$.} The noise is sized for the committed bounds, so headroom the data do not use is lost signal. After scaling by $\max|y|$, the typical $|y|$ is only $0.19$ of its bound on elevators and $0.24$ on wine. Choosing bounds that typical records come close to, and units or transforms that make this possible (for example log income), is part of using the protocol well.

\paragraph{Conditioning decides when the coefficients become usable.} The ridge is off from about $(g^\star + r_1)/\ell$ matched records (Section~\ref{sec:stats:noff}). On protein a near-collinearity involving a single feature keeps it on until $691$k records with block normalization and $13$M without, at $\varepsilon = 1$; removing that feature, a decision that public reference data can inform, lowers the threshold to $102$k.

\paragraph{Budget.} Every release, including a refused one, is charged to $\Op$'s ledger. Model search and exploratory re-runs spend the budget quickly; the protocol suits a pre-specified model per research question.

\section{Extensions and limitations}
\label{sec:ext}
% ==============================================================

\paragraph{A deviating researcher.} v3.1 makes no claim against a malicious $\Rp$. Making output privacy hold against one requires (i) zero-knowledge proofs at upload that the ciphertexts are well formed with short errors, that $b_t \in \{0, 1\}$, $\|\bxd_t\| \le B_R$, $(1 - b_t)\bxd_t = \mathbf 0$ and $\sum_t b_t \le n_R^{\max}$, together with a coarse norm bound that rules out wraparound (candidates include lattice-based and VOLE-based proofs); (ii) key generation with $\Rp$ committing first, plus proofs that its shares are short; and (iii) OPRF inputs bound to a certified cohort. A homomorphic check that relies on $\Rp$'s own decryption share cannot do this job.

\begin{remark}[Hiding membership]
\label{rem:modeM}
Replacing labeled PSI with circuit-PSI (for example VOLE-PSI with payloads~\cite{rindal2021vole}) gives both parties shares of $\bb$ and $\dXR$ in $\tau$-order, so $\Rp$ no longer learns $I$. The aggregation would move to BFV or use wide statistical masking, because shares modulo $2^\ell$ do not reduce inside CKKS. $\bA$ must then be computed under encryption and noised, and the adjacency extends to changing a record's match status, which raises the sensitivity.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Further limitations.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item Exact-identifier linkage only.
  \item Linear models only; generalized linear models via approximate sufficient statistics are future work.
  \item The variance is model-based. A sandwich variance would need a third release of $\sum_i r_i^2\bx_i\bx_i^\top$, a ciphertext--ciphertext computation with its own sensitivity.
  \item No verifiability of $\Op$'s computation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Summary and open items}
\label{sec:summary}
% ==============================================================

Protocol v3.1 links records with an OPRF and labeled PSI, aggregates under two-party threshold CKKS, and makes each release a threshold decryption in which $\Op$'s share carries the DP noise and floods the encryption error. $\Op$ never decrypts, $\Rp$ never sees a noiseless statistic that depends on $\Op$'s data, and the budget is enforced by $\Op$'s control of its share. Two releases separate the choice of the ridge from the estimate, which makes the bias theory and the intervals valid. The protocol and its guarantees are stated for any admissible parameter vector (Section~\ref{sec:params}); \textsc{Calibrate} derives the flooding parameter, the scale and the noise from it, and the ledger composes runs, fixed or adaptive, under a per-register cap.

\paragraph{Remaining obligations.}
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, label=O\arabic*.]
  \item The worst-case error bound: proven for the circuit as written, as an explicit function of the parameters ($B_{\mathrm{tot}} \approx 2^{25.3}$ at the proofs' example); proofs for the restructured circuit are open.
  \item Smudging for the discrete Gaussian, for every $\sigma_c \ge 2^{20}$ (proven), and a concrete constant-time exact sampler (open).
  \item Simulation security of threshold key generation and rotation keys against a semi-honest $\Rp$ (reduced to RLWE with circular security).
  \item The two-stage rule, the RSS correction and the coverage of both intervals, checked by simulation (Section~\ref{sec:eval:coverage}) and still to be checked on real data under v3.1.
  \item PSI parameters with explicit failure probabilities (correctness only, not charged to $\delta$), an item length that meets them at $\bar n_O = 10^6$, and a worst-case bound $B_\psi$ on the evaluation noise of the PSI replies, which \textsc{SanitizeBFV} floods.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Other open items.} A written proof of the adaptive hybrid in Proposition~\ref{prop:compose}; a sharper treatment of the smudging step (Remark~\ref{rem:sharperslack}); no-wraparound constants at the earlier levels; precise modulus-chain sizes; the free-set boundary for $\by^\top\by$ and a closed form for $d_O = 1$; explicit constants for the bootstrap RSS correction; a remedy for heteroskedasticity when clipping binds; and sixteen citation details flagged for verification in the proofs.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:bias}}
\label{app:proof}
% ==============================================================

Throughout, condition on the sample so that $\bG$, $\bc$ and $\hat{\bbeta} = \bG^{-1}\bc$ are fixed, and write $\bP = \bG^{-1}$ and $\bH = \bP\bE$.

\paragraph{Step 1: Neumann expansion on $\mathcal{E}_{\eta}$.}
On $\mathcal{E}_{\eta}$ the spectral radius of $\bH$ is at most $\eta < 1$ (Remark~\ref{rem:validity}), so
\[
  \tilde{\bbeta} = (\bG + \bE)^{-1}(\bc + \bff) = (\bI + \bH)^{-1}\bP(\bc + \bff)
  = \bigl(\bI - \bH + \bH^{2} - \bH^{3} + \bH^{4}(\bI + \bH)^{-1}\bigr)\bigl(\hat{\bbeta} + \bP\bff\bigr),
\]
and $\|\bH^{4}(\bI + \bH)^{-1}\|$ is bounded by a constant times $\|\bE\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{4}/(1 - \eta)$ in the $\bG$-norm.

\paragraph{Step 2: odd terms and the moment noise vanish.}
$\mathcal{E}_{\eta}$ is invariant under $\bE \mapsto -\bE$, $\bE$ is symmetric about zero, and $\bff$ is independent of $\bE$ and symmetric about zero. Hence $\E[\bH^{m} \mid \mathcal{E}_{\eta}] = 0$ for odd $m$, and every term containing $\bP\bff$ has zero conditional expectation. Therefore
\[
  \E[\tilde{\bbeta} \mid \mathcal{E}_{\eta}] = \hat{\bbeta} + \E[\bH^{2} \mid \mathcal{E}_{\eta}]\,\hat{\bbeta} + O(\sigma^{4}).
\]

\paragraph{Step 3: removing the conditioning.}
\[
  \E[\bH^{2} \mid \mathcal{E}_{\eta}] - \E[\bH^{2}] = \bigl(\E[\bH^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{\eta}}] - \E[\bH^{2}]\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{\eta})\bigr)/\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{\eta}),
\]
whose norm is at most $2\bigl(\E\|\bH\|^{4}\bigr)^{1/2}\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{\eta}^{c})^{1/2}/\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{\eta}) = O\bigl(\sigma^{2}e^{-p(\eta\vartheta_0 - 1)^{2}/2}\bigr)$ by Cauchy--Schwarz and Remark~\ref{rem:validity}.

\paragraph{Step 4: the second-moment contraction.}
Write $\mathbf{p} = (P_{11}, \dots, P_{pp})^{\top}$ for the diagonal of $\bP$ and $\mathbf{X} = (\chi_{ij})$.

\begin{lemma}
For any fixed symmetric $\bP$, with $\circ$ the Hadamard product,
$\E[\bE\bP\bE] = \sigma^{2}\bigl(\mathbf{X} \circ \bP + \diag(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{p}) - \PiO\diag(\mathbf{p})\bigr)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Entrywise, $\E[(\bE\bP\bE)_{ab}] = \sum_{i,j} P_{ij}\E[E_{ai}E_{jb}]$, and~\eqref{eq:noise_cov} gives $\E[E_{ai}E_{jb}] = \sigma^{2}\chi_{ai}(\delta_{aj}\delta_{ib} + \delta_{ab}\delta_{ij} - \delta_{ai}\delta_{aj}\delta_{ab})$. Summing against the three terms gives $\sigma^{2}\chi_{ab}P_{ab}$, $\sigma^{2}\delta_{ab}\sum_{i}\chi_{ai}P_{ii}$, and $-\sigma^{2}\delta_{ab}\chi_{aa}P_{aa}$. With $\chi_{aa} = \mathbf{1}[a \in O]$ these are the $(a,b)$ entries of $\mathbf{X} \circ \bP$, $\diag(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{p})$ and $-\PiO\diag(\mathbf{p})$.
\end{proof}

Writing $\chi_{ij} = 1 - (\PiR)_{ii}(\PiR)_{jj}$ turns the first two terms into projector form: $\mathbf{X} \circ \bP = \bP - \PiR\bP\PiR$ and $\diag(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{p}) = \tr(\bP)\bI - \tr(\PiR\bP)\PiR$. Left-multiplying by $\bP$ and using $\PiO\diag(\mathbf{p}) = \diag(\bP)\PiO$,
\[
  \E[\bP\bE\bP\bE] = \sigma^{2}\bigl[\bP^{2} - \bP\PiR\bP\PiR + \tr(\bP)\bP - \tr(\PiR\bP)\bP\PiR - \bP\diag(\bP)\PiO\bigr].
\]

\paragraph{Step 5: regrouping.}
Substituting $\PiO = \bI - \PiR$, the first four terms collapse to $(\bP^{2} + \tr(\bP)\bP)\PiO + (\bP\PiO\bP + \tr(\PiO\bP)\bP)\PiR$, which gives $\E[\bH^{2}] = \sigma^{2}M(\bP)$. Combining with Steps 2 and 3 proves the theorem. \qed

\paragraph{Degenerate check.}
With $\PiO = \bI$ and $\PiR = \mathbf{0}$ the formula gives $\sigma^{2}(\bP^{2} + \tr(\bP)\bP - \bP\diag(\bP))$; for $p = 1$ this is $\sigma^{2}a^{-2}\hat{\bbeta}$, matching a direct expansion of $c/(a + e)$ with $\mathrm{Var}(e) = \sigma^{2}$.

\paragraph{Extension to ridge (Theorem~\ref{thm:ridge}).}
Replacing $\bG$ by $\bG_{\lambda} = \bG + \lambda\Psi$ with $(\lambda, \Psi)$ independent of $(\bE, \bff)$ leaves $\bE$, its covariance and $\bff$ untouched, and Steps 1--5 hold verbatim with $\bP \mapsto \bP_{\lambda}$. If $\lambda$ depended on $\bE$, Step 2 would fail: $\bP_{\lambda}$ would no longer be fixed under the conditional expectation.

\section{Labeled PSI with an OPRF and record alignment}
\label{app:psi}
% ==============================================================

$\Rp$ must learn $I$ and the alignment $\pi$, while $\Op$ learns only public sizes. We use labeled PSI in the style of Chen, Laine and Rindal~\cite{chen2017fast,chen2018labeled,cong2021labeled} on OPRF values. The construction runs under BFV with $\Rp$'s own key, whose exact arithmetic modulo $t$ is needed for the zero test and label recovery. Algorithm~\ref{alg:link} gives the steps; the toy example deck works them through with numbers.

\paragraph{Pre-processing at $\Op$ (fresh every run).} $\Op$ pads its scope register to $\bar n_O$, draws a uniformly random permutation $\tau$ of its rows and a fresh OPRF key $k$. Each row $j$ becomes the item $F_k(\mathrm{id}_j)$, mapped into $\Z_t$ so as to avoid the padding root $t - 1$, with label $\tau(j)$ encrypted under a key derived from $F'_k(\mathrm{id}_j)$. Each item is hashed into all $h$ candidate bins; each bin is split into $\alpha$ partitions of at most $D$ items, where $D$ and $\alpha$ are public functions of $(\bar n_O, h, N_{\mathrm{bins}}, d_{\mathrm{cap}})$ chosen so that overflow has probability at most $\delta_{\mathrm{psi}}$. Every partition's membership polynomial is padded with the root $t - 1$ to degree exactly $D$, and its label polynomial interpolates (item, encrypted label) pairs.

\paragraph{Query at $\Rp$.} $\Rp$ pads its identifiers to $n_R^{\max}$, obtains $F_k$ and $F'_k$ of each through the OPRF, cuckoo-hashes~\cite{pagh2004cuckoo} the items into $N_{\mathrm{bins}}$ bins with the same $h$ hash functions (at most one item per bin), and sends BFV encryptions of the slot-wise powers $\mathbf{w}^{2^{i}}$, $i < W$, with $W = \lceil \log_{2} D \rceil + 1$.

\paragraph{Evaluation and reply at $\Op$.} $\Op$ forms the encryptions of $\mathbf{w}^{d}$, $d \le D$, as products of window ciphertexts, evaluates the membership and label polynomials of each partition as plaintext-coefficient linear combinations, masks with $\mathbf{r}_{1}, \mathbf{r}_{2}$ uniform in $(\Z_{t}^{\ast})^{N_{\mathrm{bins}}}$ from a CSPRNG, and returns
\[
  \textsc{SanitizeBFV}\bigl(\mathbf{r}_{1} \odot P(\mathbf{w})\bigr), \qquad
  \textsc{SanitizeBFV}\bigl(\mathbf{r}_{2} \odot P(\mathbf{w}) + L(\mathbf{w})\bigr).
\]
The first is $0$ in a bin iff $\Rp$'s item there is in $\Op$'s set, and the second is then that item's encrypted label; otherwise both are uniform. The mask $\mathbf r_1$ hides $P(\mathbf w)$ at non-matches, which would otherwise carry information about $\Op$'s items. \textsc{SanitizeBFV} and its parameters $(\kappa_\psi, B_\psi, q')$ are discussed under G4 in Section~\ref{sec:input}.

\paragraph{Recovery at $\Rp$.} For each occupied bin and partition, $\Rp$ opens the label under $F'_k$ of its item when the flag is zero, which gives $\pi(i) = \tau(j)$.

\begin{remark}[Collision probability]
A false match requires two distinct identifiers to share an item value inside a common bin. The expected number is about $n_R^{\max}\bar n_Oh/(N_{\mathrm{bins}}t)$, so $t \ge n_R^{\max}\bar n_Oh/(N_{\mathrm{bins}}\delta_{\mathrm{psi}})$ (a prime with $t \equiv 1 \bmod 2N_{B}$ for batching) keeps it below $\delta_{\mathrm{psi}}$. The padding root must be $t - 1$ for the $t$ actually used. The evaluation setting $t \approx 2^{32}$ does not meet this constraint for $\delta_{\mathrm{psi}} = 2^{-40}$: with $n_R^{\max} = 2{,}000$, $h = 3$ and $N_{\mathrm{bins}} = 2^{14}$, the expected number of false matches per run is $3.5 \times 10^{-7}$ at $\bar n_O = 4{,}096$ and $8.5 \times 10^{-5}$ at $10^6$. $\Rp$ rejects ambiguous and out-of-range labels, but an in-range false match goes undetected, so longer items are needed.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Pre-shuffle necessity]
Without $\tau$, the positions $\pi(i)$ recovered by $\Rp$ would reflect $\Op$'s original database ordering (e.g.\ by region or registration date), leaking attributes outside the DP guarantee. The fresh shuffle makes positions uniformly random. It must be applied by $\Op$ inside the protocol, not assumed of the input files.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Linkage privacy]
$\Op$ sees $n_R^{\max}$ uniformly random group elements in the OPRF and BFV ciphertexts under $\Rp$'s key in the PSI, so it learns neither $I$ nor $n_R$.
\end{remark}
